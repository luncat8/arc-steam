# 0.4.0 Wild Mine — tools plan

Derived from the wild mine draft (`0.4.0-wild-mine-draft.md`). Cave-specific rules live behind a mode guard; `0.4.0-wild-mine-plan.md` stays the reference for the cave world itself.

This plan covers the draft's improvement list in dependency order, smallest self-contained slice first.

## Draft areas

1. **Fuel: engines burn cargo resources.** No refuel station on the map; the existing mineral families feed solid, liquid and fissile engines. A finite cave liquid supply feeds the water gun and the oil engine.
2. **Bore / hammer buffer.** New item on both ends of the platform; digs tunnels 1.5× platform height at an energy cost, aimed by cursor or an angle GUI for inclined slope tunnels.
3. **Grader.** Respect the aim angle, or work in smooth mode, instead of making everything flat.
4. **Rocket fabrication on platform.** A fab module consumes minerals from cargo and produces rockets; rockets do 5× base-game damage in wild mine mode.
5. **Debris.** Heightmap-based fallen cells that can lie on any rock cell. Visual items (gold stones, unsolvable) are deferred per the draft: first the heightmap, working well.

## Slice 1 — rocket fabrication

### Decisions

**D1 — Item.** `rocket-fab` is a normal equipment item (cost, mass, research-gated behind `impact`, the same gate as `rocket-rack`). It requires an installed rack and loads the rack's tubes directly: no sell wall is needed, which is exactly what Wild Mine lacks. It works in Classic too, but is the only rocket source in Wild Mine.

**D2 — Recipe.** One cycle produces one rocket: 1 cargo cell of minerals (0.6 kg, drawn from cargo in table order) plus 2 kJ of useful energy burned by the installed engine at its efficiency through `physics.spend`, so the waste share becomes machine heat and the single energy ledger stays exact. Production ticks every 3 s while auto-fab is on; a manual `Fab 1 rocket` button fires one cycle. Stock is capped at `rackSize` (6), so a full rack stops production. Classic competitors draw from their own hopper and powertrain, outside the rig’s reserve.

**D3 — Timing.** `SteamRocketFab.step(world, dt)` is called from `physics.step` in both the wild branch and the classic branch, next to `SteamRockets.step`, behind a `root.SteamRocketFab` guard so load order never matters.

**D4 — Wild damage.** In `rockets.js stepShard`, when `physics.wildMode()` the shard's ballistic step is swept against the active cave instance's signed-distance field; contact with any cave surface spends five times its fracture energy through `cave.weaken` (gain/radius from the owner, same rule as the ball). Classic keeps `ceiling.chip` unchanged. Rocket flight through an open cave (no chamber ceiling/walls) is a known follow-up, not part of this slice.

**D5 — Readout.** One reason line only while something waits (`No rocket fab installed`, `No rocket rack`, `Rack full`, `Cargo empty · mine minerals to feed the fab`, `Reserve too low …`) plus stock/cycle totals. The fire button keeps showing rack stock.

### File map

- `js/rocket-fab.js` — new module (profiles, recipe, `setAuto`, `fab`, `step`, readout state)
- `js/rockets.js` — add `load(count, owner)`; `stepShard` applies ×5 energy in wild mode
- `js/equipment.js` — catalogue entry `rocket-fab`
- `js/physics.js` — step hook in both mode branches
- `index.html` — script tag; ROCKET FAB controls (manual button, auto checkbox, status line)
- `js/main_input.js` — bindings for the button and checkbox
- `js/main_readouts.js` — `rocketFabReadout`
- `experiments/rocket-fab.test.js` — Node tests

### Invariants to test

1. Inert without rack or fab installed; reason strings match.
2. With rack + fab + cargo + reserve: each 3 s cycle adds exactly 1 rocket to rack stock, removes exactly 1 cargo cell, and reduces the reserve by `2 kJ / efficiency`.
3. Rack full → production stops; cargo empty or flat reserve → idle with the matching reason.
4. Auto-fab persists across `reset` like other auto orders; manual fab works with auto off.
5. Wild mode: a shard's `cave.weaken` budget is 5× the same shard's energy at the same gain/radius; Classic mode untouched.
6. Energy ledger: `physics.totalEnergy` stays balanced across a fab cycle (spend books heat).

## Slice 2 — cargo fuel

Wild Mine has no charge pad and no refuelling wall. The installed engine burns
cargo minerals to hold the reserve up; Classic keeps its pad and never burns.

### Decisions

**D6 — Dry fuel is cargo, not a second commodity.** There is no dry fuel item or
new mineral row: the cargo the player already mines both sells and burns, and
the one market keeps pricing it. Cave condensate belongs to the tank, not the
hopper or the market.

**D7 — Three grades over the existing mineral table.** A grade is a fuel family,
not a single ore:

| grade | engine families | fuel minerals | chemical energy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| solid | coal, electric | stone 3.0, sand 2.0, quartz 2.5 kJ/kg | cheap host bed |
| liquid | oil | gas 40, fizzy 20, fluorite 14, vitriol 10, halite 5 kJ/kg | volatiles and hydrates |
| fissile | nuclear | iron 20, copper 25, corundum 30, silver 60, gold 80, pure gold 100 kJ/kg | reactive metals |

A cargo cell is 0.6 kg, so a stone cell carries 1.8 kJ of solid fuel and a gold
cell 48 kJ of fissile fuel. Chemical energy is stored in the reserve as input
energy; `physics.spend` still applies the engine's efficiency, so the same waste
share becomes heat as on the Classic pad. `hand` has no grade: the crew crank is
the no-fuel fallback.

**D8 — Burn target and rate.** The engine holds `world.battery` at
`physics.config.battery` (the engine-dependent `reserveNow()`), burning at
`capacity / engine.reload` joules per second. The reserve therefore refills in
the same reload seconds the Classic pad takes, and a tool that draws more than
that power runs the reserve buffer down first. Every loaded cell of the grade is
available; there is no burn button and no standing order.

**D9 — Cheapest cell per joule first.** Each burn takes the loaded fuel mineral
with the lowest live `price / (yield * cellMass)` and consumes only the mass that
the requested chemical energy needs. Ore stays in the hopper whenever a cheaper
fuel is beside it, and the market's drift changes which cell the engine prefers
without a second pricing rule.

**D10 — Ledger and mass coupling.** A burn removes cargo mass, so three terms
must reconcile:

- chemical `C` enters the reserve (any amount past capacity vents as heat);
- the removed cargo already carries potential `P = removedMass * gravity *
  cargoHeight` in `ceiling.energy`, so only `C - P` is booked as
  `world.importedEnergy`;
- on a moving platform the removed mass also carried kinetic energy
  `K = ½ removedMass * vx²` in the platform term, booked to `world.heat` exactly
  like machinery removed on the move.

`physics.totalEnergy` therefore stays constant across a burn, and the reserve
still books efficiency waste at spend time, not at burn time.

**D11 — Readout.** The reserve card names the engine, grade and remaining fuel
(cells, kJ, refill kW); the cargo card names the family and the cheapest-first
rule or the exact no-fuel reason. The status strip repeats the reason only while
the reserve is LOW or EMPTY. A dedicated help topic lists the three families.

### File map

- `js/fuel.js` — new module: grade table, readout, `burn`, `step`, per-run totals
- `js/wild-mine.js` — fuel step before the platform moves
- `js/physics.js` — reset the fuel profile with the run
- `js/main_wild.js` — reserve, cargo and status readouts
- `js/help.js`, `index.html`, `styles.css` — fuel help topic and its reserve-card mark
- `experiments/fuel.test.js` — Node tests; `experiments/fuel-probe.cjs` — balance table
- `experiments/logs/0.4.0-wild-fuel.txt` — measured opening-bed fuel table

### Invariants to test

1. Engine families map exactly to solid, liquid and fissile; `hand` maps to none.
2. A burn draws only its own family: gold survives a solid burn, stone survives a liquid or fissile burn.
3. A small burn takes the cheapest loaded fuel cell per joule first and leaves the dearer one.
4. The step adds exactly `capacity / reload` per second, stops at capacity, and burns nothing when full.
5. No matching fuel leaves the reserve alone and returns the exact reason; the hand engine never burns.
6. Classic mode never burns, even with fuel in the hopper and an empty reserve.
7. The energy ledger stays exact (≤1e-8) across a burn, at rest and while driving, including the cargo potential and moving-mass kinetic terms.
8. All three families burn their own minerals and keep the ledger exact.
9. Readout mass, chemical energy and per-run burn totals match the cargo and the live table.

## Slice 3 — water tank and the liquid cycle

**D12 — Seeps on the cave grid.** A seep is a finite, shallow pocket in a
naturally open cell above rock. Seed it from absolute cell coordinates and
original occupancy, with a guaranteed shelf pool to the right of spawn. Each
full cell holds 1 kg, 75% spray water and 25% fictional oil condensate. Keep
sparse per-chunk overrides for draws and runoff, including emptied cells, so
streaming cannot replenish a seep or pin a clean rock chunk. Seep cells are
fixed; flowing pressure, erosion and cave dissolution are separate work.

**D13 — Built-in intake.** The Wild rig starts with an empty 12 kg tank and the
intake enabled, regardless of installed tools or dry hopper fill. Only the
cave cells below the pitched hull within reach can be pumped; stop each scan
at the first solid cell. Powered suction lifts up to 2.6 kg/s for 120 J/kg
useful work, booked through `physics.spend`, and pauses at thermal cutoff. A flat
reserve allows 0.3 kg/s of hand priming, billed as crew work. A waste valve can
discard the spray water without power or loss of stored condensate, so a tank
full of water cannot lock out further refuelling.

**D14 — Separate uses, one capacity.** The existing gun takes 0.075 kg of
water per shot at its existing energy and rate; it needs the fitted tool and
never sprays condensate. Sweep its drops against the cave SDF. At a hit route
runoff to the first floor below on that side of the rock, charging any
unretained mass to drainage. Runoff contains only water, never new fuel. Oil
uses condensate first (24 kJ/kg), then follows D9 for its usual cargo minerals
at the same total refill rate. No other engine uses tank liquid. Classic keeps
its unlimited gun, brine pump and chemistry.

**D15 — Ledgers.** Track water collected as tank + sprayed + discarded, and
sprayed water as airborne + returned + drained. Condensate collected is tank +
burned. Both components count in hull inertia and tank potential at deck
height; ambient water has no mechanical ledger entry. Intake couples added
mass inelastically, spending work on the tank's potential before heat. Spray,
venting and burn book their removed mass's kinetic and tank potential as heat;
only the chemical yield is imported at burn time.

### File map and validation

- `js/wild-water.js` — seep layer, streaming overrides, tank, intake and valve.
- `js/water.js`, `js/fuel.js`, `js/physics.js`, `js/wild-mine.js` — shared gun,
  cave flight, oil burn, mass/energy integration and step ownership.
- `js/main_wild.js`, `index.html` — cave water, intake switch and tank contents.
- `experiments/wild-water.test.js` — deterministic generation, dry/blocked and
  full intake, eviction, oil restart and burn priority, runoff and balanced
  water, fuel and energy while moving. `experiments/wild-mine-interface.test.js`
  and `experiments/browser-smoke.cjs` exercise the controls and mode isolation.

## Slice 4 — aimed bore / hammer buffers

Wild Mine chambers are separated by solid host rock and variable-strength seams.
Twin percussion buffers on the rig's two noses open drivable level and sloped
corridors to new chambers, veins and seeps; Classic stays untouched.

### Decisions

**D16 — Item and placement.** `bore` (*Hammer buffers*, 620 cr, +14 kg) unlocks
behind `impact` alongside the other fracture tools, priced so the opening room's
first one or two ore loads can fund the research path and fixture. Fitting it
mounts matched percussion heads on both ends of the hull (`±platformWidth / 2`).
Because Wild Mine rigs work far from spawn once corridors open, Wild Mine also
permits engine purchases and swaps wherever the platform is stationary
(`|vx| ≤ 0.1 m/s`); Classic retains its left-shop requirement.

**D17 — Envelope and slope control.** A corridor is 1.125 m tall perpendicular
to its floor — 9 cave cells, 1.5× the 0.75 m hull-and-deck clearance height —
and projects 1.125 m ahead of the leading nose, anchored on the leading wheel's
ground contact so flat, climbing and descending floors continue without sag or
undercutting the supporting wheel. Tunnel grade is a dedicated signed elevation
in `[−platformMaxPitch, +platformMaxPitch]` (`±30°`), separate from piston tilt
so downward headings are reachable and ball launches stay undisturbed. A
cursor-aim toggle tracks the pointer's side and elevation live; moving the grade
slider returns to manual hold, and driving automatically selects the leading
end. The same grade angle is exposed for the later grader slice.

**D18 — Progressive strength-coupled cut.** Candidate solid cells inside the
active heading are ordered nearest-first along the tunnel axis, keeping the
2-cell map-floor and map-ceiling borders solid. The buffers draw up to 1.8 kW of
useful work scaled by `physics.thermalFactor`, spending 14 J of useful hammer
work per joule of remaining cell strength (17.5 J of stock-electric reserve per
strength joule: ~31 J/cell in soft halite, ~74 J/cell in host stone, ~125 J/cell
in hard corundum). Partial work persists in chunk strength and damage across
streaming, so pre-fractured rock cuts more cheaply and interrupted strokes lose
no progress. On host stone the bore pays roughly a 1.4× reserve premium per cell
over stock piston extraction (2.3× over an upgraded coil/ram/spike rig) in
exchange for a clean, drivable passage.

**D19 — Pulverised heading and exact ledger.** Opened bore cells are marked
`dug` without spawning ore fragments or cargo: corridors stay clear of
self-choking spoil at every debris-compression setting, boring cannot generate a
self-fuelling stone loop, and ore veins still reward stopping to work them with
the piston, ball or rockets. Pre-existing loose soil resting on removed cells
still settles onto the lower floor through `wild-rubble`, and natural seeps
never form on dug cells. Every joule drawn through `physics.spend` splits
between removed rock strength in `world.fractureEnergy` and percussion plus
engine waste in `world.heat`, with zero draw when the heading is already open.

**D20 — Orders, visual cue and readout.** Manual hold (`V` or the Bore button)
works the aimed end down to empty reserve; a persistent `Auto-bore ahead while
driving` checkbox engages the leading buffer whenever a travel command faces
solid rock. The Wild Mine canvas renders both fitted buffer heads with live
percussion stroke and the exact aimed corridor box from the shared geometry
helper. The control panel reports active side, grade, remaining heading cells
and estimated reserve cost to clear them, plus the exact waiting reason when
unfitted, empty, thermally cut off or clear.

- `js/wild-bore.js` — new module: aim/cursor/auto state, shared corridor geometry, progressive strength cut, ledger booking and readout
- `js/cave.js` — batched `boreCells` primitive preserving partial damage, chunk halos and single-pass SDF refresh
- `js/wild-mine.js`, `js/physics.js`, `js/equipment.js`, `js/main_progress.js` — Wild step hook before chassis motion, reset, catalogue entry and stopped-rig engine swaps in Wild Mine
- `js/main_draw.js`, `js/main_wild.js`, `js/main_input.js`, `js/main_readouts.js`, `js/help.js`, `index.html`, `styles.css` — dual end buffers, corridor guide, Wild-only controls, `V` shortcut and help topic
- `experiments/wild-bore.test.js`, `experiments/wild-bore-probe.cjs`, `experiments/logs/wild-bore-balance.txt` — Node physics/ledger/drivability tests and piston-vs-bore passage comparison

### Invariants to test

1. Inert in Classic mode and without `bore` installed; Classic controls stay hidden and Classic engine shop rule stays unchanged.
2. Corridor clears 1.125 m (9 cells, 1.5× hull clearance) perpendicular to grade on both left and right ends, preserving the supporting floor and the 2-cell world borders.
3. Rig drives through its own bored solid-rock tunnels on level (`0°`), climbing (`+25°`) and descending (`−25°`) headings in both directions without snagging.
4. Reserve spent scales strictly with remaining rock strength (corundum > stone > halite; pre-weakened rock cheaper), drops to zero in open air and stops cleanly at empty reserve or thermal cutoff.
5. Partial weakening survives chunk eviction/reload; bored cells spawn no ore yield and never seed new seeps, while soil resting above a bored cell settles onto the new floor.
6. `physics.totalEnergy` stays conserved to ≤1e-8 J across standing cuts, moving auto-bore, sloped climbs/descents and cargo-fuelled runs.
7. Auto-bore order persists across run resets; cursor aim tracks both side and grade and yields cleanly to manual slider edits.

## Later slices (decisions pending)

- **Grader.** The blade follows the platform's pitch or the bore's tunnel grade; in smooth mode it blends the heightmap instead of flattening to a single plane.
- **Debris.** `wild-rubble.js` and `wild-ball.js` handle connected-step soil heightmaps and flying fragments; the remaining work is visual pile items, which the draft defers.
