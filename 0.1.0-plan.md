# 0.1 — Energy-driven mining platform

## 0.1.1 Product and scope

Build a side-view mining simulation with a platform constrained to horizontal ground travel. Bouncing balls damage a mineral ceiling; the platform must keep balls airborne, collect falling material, and balance energy, heat, wear, and operating costs. This is not Arkanoid: do not add brick-clearing levels, lives, power-ups, paddle-angle aiming, or arbitrary speed increases.

The defining decisions are where and how hard to move, when to supply bounce energy, which falling objects to catch, and which machinery and technologies to install. A clean interactive interface must explain installed equipment, available replacements, research, and the consequences of operation.

## 0.1.2 Requirements and decisions to confirm

Confirmed by the draft:
- Separate platform regions collect debris and reflect everything that lands on them.
- Platform movement consumes energy; acceleration and braking policy affect efficiency and wear.
- Impacts and inefficient machinery create heat; cooling capacity matters.
- Ceiling cells have mineral composition, strength, and radial impact damage with distance falloff.
- Progression spans manual, coal/steam, electric, and nuclear machinery.
- Research includes improved rebound materials, trajectory prediction, wall buffers/springs, radiators, contact charging, autopilot, and mineral radar.
- Driver, mechanic, and geological expertise affect relevant operating performance.
- Economics includes mineral sales and social/political operating pressures.

Confirmed core rules:
- The platform supplies bounce energy. Balls are passive; no internal ball battery or pusher is simulated.
- Ground contact dissipates the ball's energy. The ball remains in the world and must be collected by moving the platform over it.
- The collector catches and holds balls, dissipating their mechanical energy. Recovery is not an automatic full-height relaunch.
- A low-tier drive supplies only a limited pulse of energy, and it is enabled by default. Approximately three powered contacts — the third bounce from a release — are needed to recover full ceiling height in the 42 J calibration.
- Held balls are explicitly released onto the reflector with a powered pulse; the pulse also pays the gravitational lifting cost.
- The reflector is a sprung piston whose face tilts ±10° to steer reflections: contacts and powered pushes follow the tilted face normal, each contact compresses the piston in proportion to the normal impact speed, and a damped spring extends it again as a rendered push (the collision plane stays at the rest position). The tilt is set by a slider, the mouse wheel over the chamber (about 4° per notch), or dragging in open air towards the direction the ball should go — left or up raises the angle, so the bounce follows the drag. The autopilot's level bounce moves the same writer at a 45°/s slew limit inside the ±10° range.

Confirmed mining-loop rules:
- Rock is a two-dimensional cell field. The default physical cell projects to approximately 4 × 4 px at the reference chamber scale; screen resizing does not change cell count, yield, or collision geometry.
- Rock can be inspected as elemental R/G/B composition, mineral composition, sale price, or remaining strength. Clicking a visible rock cell selects it in the inspector (no coordinate sliders) and reports its column/layer, remaining/original strength, mineral and element mix, and price; extracted/falling/caught/lost counts stay visible. Clicks low near the ground command travel instead, and open air does neither.
- Storage holds 100 extracted cells. One full load has the same mass as the 60 kg dry platform, and cargo mass participates in acceleration, momentum exchange, braking, and kinetic-energy accounting. Cargo and lifetime sales keep per-mineral masses.
- Touching either side wall automatically sells all stored cells. Both walls carry vertical `SELL` markings alongside the platform route. Each sale appends an immutable ledger entry with time, cells, mass, mineral mix, and credits.
- Balls collide with and reflect from the exposed shape of intact rock: by default a smooth heightmap polyline across columns, with an optional square-cell mode that collides against exposed faces and rounded cell corners.
- Impact damage applies 2× hit-point strength removal falling linearly to zero over a 4.5-cell (1.5×) radius; booked fracture energy is capped at the impact loss, keeping the energy ledger exact. All broken and detached cells fall as fragments without pool-limit loss.
- The asymmetric platform docks its collector into a left-wall pocket so the reflector can cover the wall all the way to `x = 0`. Pocket geometry is a fixed simulation constant, not a render effect.
- Click-to-target is base travel control: the low band of the chamber (up to the marked 1.7 m target height) sets the travel target, while rock clicks inspect. Predicted trajectories are a researched overlay; autonomous interception ships ungated as the four-duty autopilot of `autopilot.1-plan.md`, which weighs travel feasibility, energy and heat; weighing debris value, retention trade-offs and wear against the intercept remains open.

Confirmed progression and economy rules:
- Research is a prerequisite gate with explicit locked / available / researched states, a credit cost, and a measured effect of its own, so no node is a label-only unlock. Every purchasable item names the node that unlocks it, and a purchase without funds or prerequisites is refused with the reason stated.
- Installed machinery and completed research contribute deltas through the same named channels (mass, retention, pulse, cooling, radiator area, brake recovery). One installed-specification readout shows the live result that item descriptions compare against.
- Energy is bought back into the reserve at an explicit credit price up to the reserve capacity, buying only what current funds cover. Imported energy is tracked as its own term and subtracted from the total-energy balance, mirroring exported (sold, cooled) energy, so trade in either direction keeps the ledger exact.
- Sales, equipment purchases, research, and recharges share one immutable transaction ledger with time, label, and credits.

Remaining design decisions:
1. What are the final initial ball count, mine dimensions, starting equipment, and economic objective?
2. Should later cell-size options change physical resolution/yield, or resample only the view? The default remains fixed and simulation-owned.
3. Which energy era comes next (coal/steam refuelling versus electric storage), and does refuelling stay a credit purchase or become a consumable with supply, pollution, and crew consequences? The centre ground charge pad is now the confirmed free, positional refill for the current electric era; the credit import remains only as an emergency resupply.

Current sandbox calibration:
- The ball uses a 1.8× flight-time slowdown: effective inertia is 3.24 kg and its gravity is 9.81 / 3.24 m/s². This scales free-flight speed to two-thirds while preserving weight, energy at a given height, launch height for a given pulse, and the total energy budget. Debris and the chamber retain 9.81 m/s² gravity.
- The chamber is 24 m wide × 15 m high with the 2 m rock layer raised to 13–15 m and a 4 m charge-pad strip centred at x = 12. A drive pulse supplies up to 42 J useful energy at 80% efficiency (a 52.5 J debit), sized so the third bounce from a release reaches the ceiling. The 60 kg platform has a 400 N movement-force limit and a 10 m/s speed limit — two rounded 1.5× steps from the original 180 N / 7 m/s tuning — and a shared 12 kJ reserve.
- The stronger drive raised the scripted policy's average draw from about 77 W to about 219 W, so a full reserve now lasts roughly a minute of aggressive travel. Reserve energy is bought back at 25 cr/kJ (300 cr for a full charge) against a measured gross income of roughly 330–620 cr per shift, which makes smooth driving and braking recovery economically visible instead of free.
- The technology tree costs 2040 cr in total (180 / 240 / 300 / 340 / 460 / 520 cr) and the six equipment items cost 2800 cr (300 / 250 / 400 / 300 / 650 / 900 cr), so completing both is a multi-shift goal rather than a single-trip purchase.
- The ballistic computer predicts a full chamber flight ahead (horizon `2√(2·ceiling / ballGravity) + 1` seconds) in 0.01 s marches, sampling into fixed buffers, and stops at the first rock contact, deck contact, ground contact, or horizon; wall and ceiling reflections follow the simulation's own rules, including retention at the ceiling. A typical prediction costs 5–35 µs, so recomputing it at the 10 Hz readout cadence is free next to the 240 Hz simulation (about 11 µs per step).
- The thermal reservoir starts at 20 °C with 300 J/K capacity, 1.1 W/K base convective conductance, 0.08 m² radiating area, and 0.85 emissivity. Powered movement and bounce pulses derate linearly from 85 °C to a 135 °C cutoff; braking remains available. The 650 cr radiator array adds 8 kg, 0.35 m² of exposed area, and corresponding convective cooling.
- The autopilot adds four ungated duties over the manual controls — chase (`X`), timed intercept (`shift`+`X`), drive keeper (`Z`), level bounce (`C`) — each yielding to the manual command that does the same job. Measured on one seeded 120 s shift: the timed intercept draws 6182 J against the chase's 13057 J for the same 78 m of travel, and 5277 J (150.8 J per bounce) with the brake flywheel installed; the drive keeper holds the ball's apex at 13.1–13.7 m against the 9.82 m ceiling line where manual play reaches 24.4 m; the level bounce leaves mean post-bounce horizontal speed at 0.00–0.14 m/s while slewing at its 45°/s limit. `experiments/logs/autopilot.1-trips.txt` holds the table.
- The centre charge pad is ground type, not a purchase: standing still on it (`|vx| < 0.1 m/s`) imports 3 kW, filling the 12 kJ reserve in exactly 4 s. Imported energy is booked like a bought recharge, so the ledger stays exact. The paid 25 cr/kJ import stays as an emergency rescue for a platform that cannot reach the pad.
- A held ball has zero tracked mechanical energy in its storage state; lifting it to the release height is charged on release. The held icon on the deck denotes storage, not free gravitational potential.
- Use normalized composition weights and fixed physical cell dimensions for mining; treat screen scale independently.

## 0.1.3 Runtime and architecture

- Plain HTML/CSS/JavaScript; classic scripts, no build step, no runtime network dependency, usable through `file://`.
- Vendor any required library locally with its license. Use a seeded local simplex-noise implementation for mineral generation.
- Keep simulation independent of the DOM and renderer. Guard Node exports for numerical and deterministic tests.
- Suggested files: `index.html`, `styles.css`, `js/config.js`, `js/noise.js`, `js/materials.js`, `js/world.js`, `js/physics.js`, `js/platform.js`, `js/ceiling.js`, `js/economy.js`, `js/research.js`, `js/prediction.js`, `js/render.js`, `js/ui.js`, `js/main.js`.
- Use a fixed simulation step with a bounded accumulator; rendering must not change physical outcomes. Pause safely on tab suspension rather than simulating an unbounded backlog.
- Use typed arrays for ceiling fields and pooled arrays for moving bodies, fragments, and prediction samples. Avoid allocations in simulation/render loops.
- Separate tuning data, installed equipment, research unlocks, and transient world state. Technology availability is not equivalent to installation.
- Add files as their responsibilities become necessary rather than building empty abstractions up front.

## 0.1.4 Physics and accounting

Use meters, seconds, kilograms, joules, watts, and a documented temperature model internally; convert only at display boundaries.

- For a passive bounce retaining 90% of normal kinetic energy, use normal restitution `sqrt(0.9)`, not `0.9`. Rebound height is then 90% for a stationary horizontal surface.
- Calculate collision energy in the appropriate moving-surface frame. A drive impulse must debit its source according to efficiency and deposit waste heat explicitly.
- Track fuel/battery energy, kinetic and gravitational energy, stored spring/flywheel energy, thermal energy, energy exported by cooling, and deliberate sinks such as fractured material. Keep diagnostics for unexplained energy creation.
- Represent machinery temperature as ambient temperature plus stored thermal energy divided by heat capacity. Cool by both convection and Stefan–Boltzmann radiation; book removed heat as an explicit exported-energy term. At the thermal limit, reduce powered movement force and bounce-pulse output, while preserving full braking force.
- Tune thermal capacity, base cooling, derate onset/cutoff, and radiator area from measured operation. Installed radiators must affect the heat-transfer calculation rather than act as a label-only upgrade.
- Treat the draft's battery/runtime figures as illustrative estimates, not balancing constants. Use `t = sqrt(2h/g)` for fall time and the full up/down cycle when estimating steady bounce power; cycle time changes as height changes.
- Integrate platform force, mass, speed, traction/force limits, and braking. Manual direction commands request engine force or braking according to current velocity.
- Apply regeneration only when suitable storage exists, with capacity and efficiency limits. Low braking can use the flywheel when installed; excess must have an explicit dissipation path.
- Resolve fast ball/fragment contacts against platform regions, individual exposed rock-cell faces, and walls without tunneling. Reflect the ball from the contacted face normal; use swept collision queries or justified bounded substeps.
- Model engine inefficiency, impact heating, radiative/convective cooling, and documented overheating effects. For a 20% efficient engine, 80% of input energy becomes waste heat.
- Derive wear from load, impacts, braking, and temperature using visible tuning parameters; apply relevant crew modifiers once.

Acceptance: deterministic free fall and passive bounce tests; bounce-height ratio; drive energy budget; force-limited travel; braking/regeneration capacity; heat balance; collision tests at cell boundaries and maximum supported speeds.

## 0.1.5 Ceiling, minerals, and fragments

- Generate three seeded two-dimensional smooth-noise fields representing elemental proportions; map them through a documented function to normalized mineral weights.
- Use a fixed 192 × 16 rock grid over the 24 m × 2 m starting rock layer (`0.125 m` cells, approximately `4 × 4 px` at reference scale). Derive initial strength and sale composition from material tables. Store remaining strength separately from original strength.
- Apply radial impact damage with explicit radius (4.5 cells), 2× hit-point strength removal, and linear falloff from the hit point to zero at the radius edge. Avoid damage depending on render scale or frame rate.
- Remove failed and detached column cells and emit pooled debris with conserved material quantities so no broken cells are lost and all broken mass falls. Aggregate fragments where needed if all pool slots are occupied, preserving mass, cell count, mineral value, and energy.
- Render and collide the rock as a smooth heightmap by default, with an optional square-cell shape mode. Render selectable element R/G/B, mineral, price, and remaining-strength views. Radar later limits what information those views reveal beyond scanned depth rather than changing simulation data.
- Store at most 100 cells (`0.6 kg` each); a full load doubles the dry platform mass. Catching transfers fragment momentum into platform/cargo motion and dissipates the non-conserved collision energy as heat.
- Sell the complete cargo automatically at either side wall, preserving a transaction total and exported mechanical-energy term.

Acceptance: repeatable seeds, normalized compositions, reproducible damage falloff, exposed-face reflection, no duplicate cell extraction, conserved extracted/caught/lost/sold mass, a hard storage limit, correct cargo-dependent acceleration, and bounded fragment counts.

## 0.1.6 Controls and predictive assistance

- `A`/`D`: manual horizontal movement, selecting engine/brake behavior from current motion.
- `W`/`S`: adjust engine/brake force setting; display the setting and flywheel involvement.
- `Space` / `Q`: toggle upward bounce drive without activating focused GUI controls; show enabled state and actual energy use separately.
- Click the playfield: request a horizontal target position under the agreed automation policy.
- Manual commands override target following. Blur focused buttons, sliders, or selects on game shortcuts so `Space` and other shortcuts never re-trigger GUI elements.
- Ballistic-computer research enables dotted trajectories, predicted landing location/time, and a platform ghost at that time.
- Predictions use the same gravity, collision, drive, and movement constraints as the simulation. Mark uncertainty or stop prediction at unknown ceiling interactions rather than showing a misleading guaranteed trajectory.
- Autonomous interception considers travel feasibility, ball retention, debris value, energy, heat, and wear. Do not merely chase the nearest impact at maximum acceleration: the timed intercept spends the predicted flight time arriving (rolling through the mark when that is the slower run), tightens its braking deadband when recuperation exists, keeps the pulse armed only while the passive bounce would fall short of the ceiling, and levels the bounce through the piston's own slew limit. An unreachable mark is reported as a shortfall in metres, not chased at full thrust.
- The autopilot is four checkboxes with `X` / `shift`+`X` / `Z` / `C` shortcuts and one status line with a clause per engaged duty. Only one intercept duty is engaged at a time, and each duty disengages when the matching manual command arrives (`A`/`D` or a travel click, `Space`/`Q` or the drive button, slider/wheel/drag).

Acceptance: keyboard and pointer controls, consistent force settings, manual override, infeasible-intercept indication, and prediction error measured against actual impacts in controlled scenes.

## 0.1.7 Equipment, progression, and economy

- Define equipment data with mass, size, force/power, efficiency, thermal limits, wear, cost, and compatibility where relevant.
- Make installed configuration affect platform geometry and acceleration; preview changes before purchase/installation.
- Introduce manual, steam, electric, and nuclear energy systems incrementally through a shared force/energy/heat interface, while keeping their sources and operational constraints distinct.
- Implement rebound materials, wall energy buffers, radiators, flywheel storage, batteries, and contact charging as real simulation components, not label-only upgrades.
- Contact charging requires touching a built rail; ball/debris strikes can damage it. Placement and protection must matter.
- Research has explicit prerequisites, costs, locked/available/researched states, and equipment unlocks. Keep numerical balance in data tables.
- Driver experience modifies fuel use and wear; mechanic experience modifies wear and repair; geological expertise modifies scan depth and excavation effectiveness.
- Track collected composition, storage, sale prices, demand, purchases, repairs, and net operating income with a transaction ledger.
- Add pollution-driven coal taxation and nuclear-related worker health/productivity/strike pressures only after the base economy is measurable. Expose causes and consequences; avoid unexplained random penalties.

Acceptance: no purchase without funds/prerequisites, no duplicate charges, correct installed-vs-unlocked state, conserved storage/sale quantities, and testable crew/policy modifiers.

## 0.1.8 Interface

- Central mine canvas with readable ball, fragment, collector, reflector, and target markers.
- Compact operating HUD: energy/fuel, drive status, force, speed, temperature, wear, cargo, and funds. Show units and limiting conditions.
- Inspectable equipment panel comparing installed and available parts, including mass and operating trade-offs.
- Interactive technology tree with prerequisites, costs, current states, and unlock explanations.
- Market/cargo panel, crew panel, and contextual warnings. Avoid forcing the player through every panel during active interception.
- Pause and simulation-speed controls for inspection/testing; document whether modal purchases pause the simulation.
- Responsive layout, legible contrast, keyboard-accessible HTML controls, and status text beyond color alone. Keep dense interface text in DOM rather than painting all controls into the canvas.
- Wide-screen layout, four rows: system bar (clock, pause, speed, new game, world options such as rock shape); full-width telemetry; play row of technology research | chamber | platform controls | mineral inspector; economy row of equipment | cargo | market. The chamber sets the play row height and side columns scroll inside it, so the canvas never stretches to the tallest list. Narrower screens move research and the inspector under the chamber, then stack everything with controls directly after the chamber.
- Under the chamber: the collector/reflector legend and the rock view switch. The view switch is a radio group (one label per mode wrapping its radio) with a single writer `setViewMode(id)` that returns early when unchanged, otherwise updates the mode and marks the scene dirty. Click and arrow keys arrive as `change`; `pointerover` walks up to the label and selects its radio, but only when `(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)` matches, so touch stays click-only. The hover listener allocates nothing. The renderer reads the mode variable, never the DOM.
- Hover switching is for display state only. Options that change physics (rock shape) stay click-only, so a passing pointer cannot change the game.
- The canvas redraws after any simulation step or when a paused scene changes (view mode, selection, target, drive toggle, purchase, resize, shape), not every animation frame.
- The readout cadence may rewrite telemetry freely, but every other DOM write is change-only: unchanged text, attributes, row states, slider positions and disabled flags are never assigned again.
- Blocked upgrade buttons use `aria-disabled` rather than `disabled`, so a click still reaches the handler and the refusal states its reason (funds, prerequisite, already owned).
- The autopilot block is four label-wrapped checkboxes with the shortcut on the right, coloured through `:has(:checked)`, plus a status paragraph hidden while nothing is engaged. Checkboxes, keys and manual overrides share one writer pair so the box always shows the live duty state, and the status text is assembled only in the readout cadence through the change-only writers.

## 0.1.9 Delivery sequence and validation gates

1. **Physics sandbox:** fixed-step loop, manual motion, one passive ball, platform zones, bounce-drive accounting, minimal diagnostic HUD. Gate: physical invariants and controls pass Node tests and a `file://` smoke test.
2. **Mining loop:** seeded ceiling, mineral strength, impact damage, fragments, collection, cargo and basic sales. Gate: repeatable mining and mass/value accounting.
3. **Operational trade-offs:** fuel/battery budgets, heating/cooling, wear/repair, component mass, braking and flywheel. Gate: contrasting driving policies produce explainable measured costs.
4. **Usable management UI:** installed/available equipment, comparisons, purchases, research prerequisites, and clear operating feedback. Gate: complete mine–collect–sell–upgrade loop without developer controls.
5. **Assistance and infrastructure:** ballistic predictions, autonomous interception (shipped ungated in autopilot.1, measured in `experiments/logs/autopilot.1-trips.txt`), radar, springs, and vulnerable charging rails. Gate: predictions validated against actual trajectories and technology locks enforced.
6. **Progression and society:** remaining energy eras, crew progression, market demand, pollution taxes, nuclear workforce effects. Gate: each era has distinct measurable trade-offs and no free energy/money exploits.
7. **Tuning and performance:** representative seeded scenes across supported cell resolutions and object counts. Gate: stable simulation, bounded memory, documented hardware/results and budgets; no premature fixed performance claim.

Place Node test/measurement scripts under `experiments/`; keep useful results under `experiments/logs/`. Cover deterministic replay, collision edge cases, energy/mass accounting, unlock transactions, and prediction accuracy. Manually test direct-file startup, resize, keyboard focus, tab suspension, and ordinary static HTTP serving.

## 0.1.10 Non-goals and next decision

No multiplayer, backend, build pipeline, external asset service, or unrelated genre mechanics are required. Persistence, audio, and additional game modes are not part of the initial scope unless separately requested.

Next design decision: choose the next energy era and its resupply model. The reserve is now a per-minute consumable at the current drive response, so coal/steam refuelling (fuel mass, burn rate, pollution, and crew consequences) or electric storage (capacity, charge rate, mass) has to be defined before further economy tuning. Autonomous interception already ships ungated per `autopilot.1-plan.md`; after the era decision, wear accounting and debris-aware interception (weighing fragment value against keeping the bounce) follow the rules above.
