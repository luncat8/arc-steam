The model (short)
  • Water is a G+B compound (crystal non-metal + gas, the H2O of this world): polar, kin to gas B, hungry for metal R, unable to break the G-framework.
  • Solubility is a class in the mineral table, like strength: instant (Rock salt, new), fast (Gas-bearing rock), slow (Fluorite), none (silicates, quartz, corundum, native metals, sand). A cell dissolves per mineral, mixture-weighted, the same way strengthInto() weighs strength.
  • Dissolution is selective extraction: water strips the soluble mineral out of the mix; the insoluble skeleton keeps its own strength and crumbles through the existing debrisLimit only when undermined.
  • Water saturates: a parcel of water dissolves only up to its capacity, so fresh flow cuts and standing puddles stall.
  • Erosion is a separate, mechanical process: flowing water entrains loose grains above a speed threshold and releases them when it slows. A carried grain keeps its mineral, mass and value; it never dissolves by being carried (only soluble grains in contact with water still dissolve chemically).
  • The jet is weak: droplets book impact energy (default 0.05 J vs 3–7.8 J/cell strengths) — cranking the slider shows what real chipping would take; dissolution is what water is for.
