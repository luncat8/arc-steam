# 0.3.6 — Solubility model (water line, companion to the water draft)

## The idea in one line

Water is the world's G+B compound — and dissolution is a **per-mineral class in
the mineral table**, weighed into a cell the same way `strengthInto()` weighs
strength: water strips the soluble mineral out of a mix, the insoluble skeleton
keeps its own strength, and the existing `debrisLimit` decides when the
undermined residue crumbles to grains.

## Why water behaves this way (the G+B story)

- The world binds matter three ways: **metallic R–R** (native silver, gold),
  **framework G–G** (the strongest bonds there are — which is why quartz at
  6.0 and corundum at 7.8 J/cell top the strength table), and **salt pairs
  R··G**, simple ionic lattices that often trap gas B in their voids.
- Water is a polar **G+B molecule** (the H2O of this world: crystal non-metal
  plus gas). Its B-pole reaches for metal R, its G-bridges cannot break a
  G-framework, and it is chemically at home with trapped B.
- So water: never touches framework silicates, quartz, corundum, iron oxides
  or native metals; pulls salt pairs apart; and seeps into gas-bearing lattices,
  displacing the B, which leaves as bubbles while the lattice collapses to mud.

## The rule

1. **Table change, nothing else moves.** Every mineral gets a `sol` class:
   `none` (stone, iron, copper, quartz, corundum, silver, gold, sand — sand is
   loose but chemically inert), `slow` (fluorite, already B-dominant at
   b 0.62–0.98), `fast` (gas-bearing rock), `instant` (the new Rock salt).
   Ranges, strengths, prices stay as calibrated.
2. **One new mineral: Rock salt** (`NaCl` analog) occupying the one empty zone
   of the range table — balanced R and G at a simple salt stoichiometry, almost
   no trapped gas: `r 0.40–0.72, g 0.34–0.66, b 0.02–0.28`, `strength 1.2`
   (holds above `debrisLimit`, so removal is dissolution, not collapse),
   evaporite beds, `demandMean 0.15` (cheap industrial commodity).
3. **Cell rule.** A wet cell loses soluble mass per mineral:
   `dm = mass × rate(class) × wetness × (1 − load/capacity) × dt`. The mix and
   its `strengthInto()` shrink together; when the effective strength crosses
   `debrisLimit`, the residue collapses to grains through the ordinary fall
   path. Cells that finish dissolving simply become empty — no debris.
4. **Saturation.** A parcel of water dissolves only up to its capacity
   (default 60 g per water unit). Fresh flow cuts, brine stalls, standing
   puddles eat their support until saturated. Downstream soluble rock survives
   a saturated stream.
5. **Grains have surface.** A loose grain dissolves at `rate × 3` (surface
   area), so crushed salt disappears fast — but only if soluble; quartz sand
   grains never dissolve, they only travel.

## The two water processes, kept strictly apart

- **Dissolution (chemical, one-way):** mass leaves the rock as dissolved load
  in actual water parcels (droplets never carry load; flows and the pool do).
  It books per mineral, to the gram, and leaves with the water (drained) or
  precipitates as a crust when a parcel dries out. Gas rock splits its mass:
  85% leaves as B-gas (bubbles, ties into the gas-caves line), 15% rides the
  water.
- **Entrainment (mechanical, reversible):** flowing water above a speed
  threshold lifts loose grains; when the flow slows below the release
  threshold, the grain is dropped. A carried grain keeps its mineral, mass and
  value — being carried never dissolves anything. The weak jet (default
  0.05 J per droplet against 3–7.8 J/cell strengths) chips nothing; its job
  is to deliver water, not force.

## The lab

`experiments/solubility-lab.html` — single file, no deps, seeded field with
salt beds, sand beds, gas pockets and the familiar veins. It measures, live:

- per-mineral dissolved mass and rate (table + strip chart by class),
- grains spawned / carried / resting, entrainment and release thresholds,
- the exact mass ledger: rock left + dissolved (drained, in water, crust, gas)
  + grain mass = initial, checked on screen every refresh,
- two probes: **Jet** (aimed stream, hold LMB; RMB pours; the gameplay case)
  and **Beaker** (Fill/Drain immerses the sample; bulk-rate and saturation
  curves), plus Color / Strength / Solubility views and live sliders for every
  rate above. A `window.__lab` handle exposes `snapshot()` for scripted probes.
