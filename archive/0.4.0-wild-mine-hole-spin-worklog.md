# 0.4.0 Wild Mine — ball stuck in small holes spinning forever

## 1. Review of the friction sleep fix

The previous milestone stopped a wall stealing the floor contact: if the floor
still supports the centre the floor tangent keeps Coulomb friction and rolling
resistance, and a motor-off ball with both centre and rim speed under
`restSpeed` sleeps. That fixed a ball leaning against the start-room wall.

The player's follow-up report: the ball still gets stuck in small holes and
spins forever. Reproduction with dug pits in the shelf floor confirmed two
gaps the wall fix does not cover.

### Gap A — rim support has no floor under the centre

A ball wedged on the rim corners of a narrow pit (or on cell-scale rims on a
rough cave floor) is held by two side contacts, but `floorBelow` scans the
centre column, which is open through the pit, so the "supported" test
(`floorGap <= contactSlop`) fails. The ball micro-bounces: every frame is
`airStep`, `contact` stays `CONTACT_NONE`, and rolling resistance and the
`restSpeed` sleep — both of which live only in the supported `rollStep` path —
never run. A spun ball on the rim then keeps `ω` essentially forever.

### Gap B — micro-bounces skip the supported resolution almost every frame

On cell-corner rims a 5–8 cm bounce is a "hit" (`curvatureExceeds` always
exceeds on a sharp corner), so the ball leaves the ground every landing with
`e = sqrt(0.9)` and lands back a few frames later. Rolling resistance — the
only dissipation a no-slip roll has (slip friction does nothing at
`v_t = ω r`) — was applied only in `rollStep`, so a rim-rattling ball kept
its horizontal coast for minutes: pit <-> rim <-> pit slosh with no horizontal
drain. The vertical bounce itself decays geometrically and eventually lands in
the sub-`bounceFloor` inelastic zone; the horizontal part did not decay at all
on near-flat rim hits.

### Detour found while testing — the ball mines its own shaft

Under the fix the synthetic pit cases now resolve, except a ball dropped into
a deep pit can mine the pit bottom open (D3 damage is contact-force driven)
and follow the shaft it carves down through the rock section. That is gravity
re-feeding the ball through its own mining, i.e. emergent gameplay, not a
contact-resolution defect; energy is booked either way (`drift ≈ 1e-10 J`).
Left as is; noted for the player.

## 2. What was implemented (`js/wild-ball.js`)

- **Ground memory** (`groundMemoryTime = 0.5 s`): every resolving rock contact
  (`resolveContact`) or supported frame (`rollStep`) refreshes a short window
  on the ball — its tangent and the normal. The window keeps the strongest
  load-bearing share seen inside it (`max` of `ny > 0`), so a sideways wall
  graze cannot poison a floor normal; a ceiling/sideway face stores `0` and
  yields no authority.
- **Carried rolling resistance** (`carryGround`): while the window is fresh,
  rolling resistance keeps acting along the cached ground tangent on pure
  flight frames — `rollingResistance * ny * dt` with the same
  `F = μ_rr N` model as the on-land coast. It never reverses `v_t`, couples a
  no-slip roll (`ω` follows `v_t / r`), and books the loss as heat through
  `bookMech`. It is deliberately not applied on the bounce frame
  (`resolveContact` resolved an impulse this frame): that impulse belongs to
  restitution and friction, and applying drag there disturbed the exact
  angular-momentum-about-contact invariant.
- The window drag does not snap the ball to sleep itself — a low-apex bounce
  is not a rest. Rest remains owned by the inelastic capture (sub-`bounceFloor`
  landing → normal velocity zeroed, floor-like contact) followed by `rollStep`
  → `restSpeed` sleep, exactly as before.

`resolveContact` now reports whether it resolved an impulse; `airStep` applies
`carryGround` only on flight frames (no contact resolved). The bounce frame is
physically unchanged.

## 3. Validation

- `experiments/wild-mine.test.js` — new regression test
  `M3 small holes: a spun ball wedged on a pit rim sleeps instead of spinning
  forever`: ball wedged on the rim corners of a 1-cell-deep, 2-cell-wide pit
  with `ω = 6`, motor off. Before: `contact` never reaches `CONTACT_ROCK`.
  After: sleeps on the rim at t ≈ 1.6 s, drift `< 1e-8`.
- `M3 bounce energy recycling` passive-reference window widened to 45 s: the
  motor-off superball legitimately needs ~34 s of sub-radius rebounds before
  the apex capture (it is the design's superball); the assertion ratio is
  unchanged. One stale threshold relaxed: recycled settle tangent `-0.7 → -0.55`
  (rolling resistance, now integrated, bleeds some coast; sign and most of the
  roll are preserved).
- Synthetic pit sweep ( ω₀ = 6): 1×1, 2×1, 3×1, 1×2, 2×2, 4×2, 6×2, 6×4, 2×3,
  3×3, 8×3 pit cells dug in the shelf. All slosh to rest in ≤ 10 s; all end
  `|v| < restSpeed`, `|ω r| < restSpeed`, `CONTACT_ROCK`, drift ≤ 1e-10, except
  the 8×3 case which follows the mined shaft downward (gameplay, see §1).
- Bitwise trace of the passive vertical drop: first divergence at frame 5072
  (t = 21.1 s) is the drag removing a −0.093 m/s horizontal drift the old code
  kept forever — the fix working, not a regression.
- `node --test` over every `experiments/*.test.js` / `*.test.cjs`: 484 / 484 pass
  (34 files), plus `cave`/`physics`/`balls`/`wild-mine-interface`/`collapse-probe`
  re-run together: 81 pass.
- Browser smoke not run: playwright is not available in this environment; the
  page module wiring is covered by `experiments/wild-mine-interface.test.js`.
