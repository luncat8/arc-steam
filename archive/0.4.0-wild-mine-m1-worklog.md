# 0.4.0 Wild Mine — review of 0.3.13, plan, and milestone M1

## 1. Review of the previous task (0.3.13)

`js/tool-telemetry.js` builds one `cr/kJ` summary per readout tick and the page
renders it beside each tool. Reading it against `js/physics.js`:

- **Bug fixed — a machine was addressed by the world.** `measureBall` ended with
  `physics.machineDrive(world, id)`, but `machineDrive(machine)` reads
  `machine.drive`. The call therefore read `world.drive`, which is `true` for the
  whole run, so *every* machine's ball row claimed to be active — a parked rival
  included. It now passes `machine`. The readout gates the yield tone on
  `entry.active`, so a rival parked at its mark was showing a green yield.
- Verified non-bug: `out.dry = sand.feed(world, id) < 0` looks like it should
  feed a grain. `feed` only scans the hopper for the next abrasive and returns
  its index, so the readout has no side effect.

  "the drive is engaged". The sandblaster is fired by its own switch, so the
  drive flag is not its gate.

reads `ball.active === false`, and toggling the rig's `world.drive` moves the

## 2. Plan

`0.4.0-wild-mine-plan.md` is the new standalone artifact. It resolves the four
open questions in the draft and adds the constraints the draft left implicit:

- **Ball battery (draft: "platform supply? atomic?").** Onboard battery, charged
  by induction only while the ball is on the platform deck; the platform's
  reserve pays. One ledger, and P1 keeps a reason to exist between shots.
- **"Damage from contact force".** The normal impulse decides *whether* a contact
  is a hit, the lost normal kinetic energy is the fracture energy that
  `weaken` receives, and the retained 90% survives the bounce. This is exactly
  the chamber's own split, so damage numbers stay comparable across modes.
- **Rolling vs hit.** A no-slip friction impulse exchanges `v` and `ω = I·ω` at
  fixed energy; a contact is a roll while the normal turns less than 30° across
  one ball diameter and the normal speed stays under 0.35 m/s. A convex corner
  tighter than the radius is always a hit, which is the draft's "sharp
  curvature acts as a wall".
- **Vertical scale.** The draft asks for a log law with a 0.25× far edge. The
  plan uses the reciprocal law `1/(1+3u)` instead: the same far-field shape, but
  its integral — the drawing map — is closed-form, so the drawn position and its
  inverse both exist in a few lines, like the chamber's cubic warp.
- **Debris compression.** `setFloorCompression` already rounds to half steps in
  `1..8`, so `1.5` only needed the `<option>` it was missing; the selector now
  offers it in every mode.
- Milestones M1–M6, the invariants each one is tested against, and the file
  layout.

## 3. M1 — the cave field (`js/cave.js`)

The chamber's one-heightmap-per-column model cannot hold a cave wall, an
overhang or debris on a ledge. `js/cave.js` replaces it with one 2D occupancy
grid per 16 m chunk plus a signed distance field derived from it.

- **Generation** is a pure function of `(seed, absolute column, row)`: two
  flattened value-noise octaves for chambers (freq 0.02 / 0.054, threshold 0.72)
  and a ridged field for veins (freq 0.035 × 2.2 in y, threshold 0.9). The
  section is closed by two rows of rock at `y = 0` and `y = 64`. A start room of
  6.4 × 3.8 m (25% of the classic 25.8 × 15 m view) is carved at `(0, 32)` with a
  noise-eroded rim, over an 8 m flat shelf with 2.4 m of headroom — three times
  the platform's 3.6 m span requirement.
- **Strength** comes from the same two geology calls the chamber's
  `populateCell` makes (`fillElements` + `fillMix` → `materials.strengthInto`),
  so the rock is the same rock. It is read lazily per cell with a `sampled` bit:
  the shared sampler costs ~2.5 µs per cell, which would be ~250 ms of work for
  every fresh chunk, while generation only needs occupancy.
- **Occupancy, strength and damage stay separate arrays**, and a `dug` bit
  records rock removed by mining as distinct from generated void. No boundary is
  immune: a cave wall is damaged by the same `weaken` rule as the roof.
- **SDF**: two exact squared-Euclidean transforms (Felzenszwalb 1D lower
  envelope, row pass then column pass), one for solid and one for void, over a
  chunk padded by the band (8 cells = 1 m). Distances are centre-to-centre, so
  half a cell is taken off: the zero level lands on the cell boundary a body
  collides with instead of half a cell inside the rock. Normals are the
  normalised central-difference gradient, computed on demand.
- **Edits** refresh a `bandCells`-wide rectangle around the damaged patch in
  every chunk it touches, by Euclidean ring search clipped at the band — the
  same numbers the chunk-wide transform stores, seam included.
- **Queries** (all allocation-free, caller scratch): `solidAt`, `sdfAt`,
  `normalAt`, `traceCircle` (sample + 12-step bisection: a wall, an overhang and
  a ledge answer through one call), `floorBelow`, `roofAbove`, `spanAt`,
  `weaken`, `dig`, `strengthAt`, `damageAt`, `mixAt`.
- **Streaming**: live chunks for the camera window, a one-entry chunk memo for
  trace hot paths, a `maxNew` budget after the first fill, and an `edited` set —
  an evicted chunk that was mined is stored and restored, an untouched one is
  regenerated from its seed.
- Damage follows the chamber's rule with gravity as the axis: a cell with rock
  *below* it is held at `heldCellShare` instead of leaving, so a fall always
  starts at a free face.

Measured in node: a warm 16 m × 64 m chunk generates in ~15 ms (45 ms for the
first, cold), a chunk holds ~1.5 MB of typed arrays, and a `dig` patch refresh
costs about 0.4 ms.

## 4. Validation

```
node --check js/cave.js
node --test experiments/cave.test.js            13 pass
node --test experiments/tool-telemetry.test.js   6 pass (1 new)
node --test experiments/interface.test.js       56 pass
node --test experiments/debris-compression.test.js experiments/depth-scale.test.js experiments/ceiling.test.js
                                                37 pass
```

The cave tests assert determinism per seed (and difference between seeds), the
solid/void share of the section, the SDF against an independent brute-force
reference in three chunks and across a seam, unit normals and the floor normal,
a dropped ball landing exactly one radius above `floorBelow`, an inside-rock
start being pushed out at once, `weaken` spending no more than its energy and
holding supported rock, a free face releasing only after its support is dug, a
patch refresh equalling a rebuild, streaming keeping an edited chunk through
eviction, and `mixAt` shares summing to one.
