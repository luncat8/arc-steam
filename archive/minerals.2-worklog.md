# minerals.2 — Element field, mineral lab and a calibrated table

## Review of the plan as it stood

The plan was sound on everything it separated: absolute element
concentrations, one geological table, bounded membership instead of a
nearest-box softmax, strength in joules independent of price, abundance
pricing with seeded demand. Three things in it could not work as written, and
one was missing.

- **The element distribution made width-based rarity meaningless.** Value noise
  is bilinear interpolation of hashed lattice points, so its values pile up
  around 0.5: measured mean 0.500, sd 0.215, 4.0% of cells per 0.025-wide bin
  at the centre against 0.16% at the edges — a 25:1 spread. A band of width
  0.10 near 0.5 covers 8% of cells, the same band at 0.90 covers 1.2%. The
  plan's rare ores sit precisely at the extremes: native silver
  `R 0.90…1.00, G 0…0.10, B 0…0.10` and gold `R 0.90…1.00` occupy about
  0.0007 and 0.0017 of the field. They were never going to be found.
- **The product of three memberships quietly punishes three-condition ores.**
  A cell that is 80% matched on all three channels scores 0.51; a one-condition
  ore at 80% scores 0.80. Every ore in the table has three conditions except
  gas-bearing rock, so the ores lost to each other and to the host by roughly
  the cube of their partial matches.
- **A broad box swallows a narrow one.** When one signature's box lies inside
  another's, both score 1 everywhere in the narrow box, so the narrow ore can
  never lead a cell — it only ever ties. Widening ranges to gain abundance (the
  only knob the plan offered) makes this worse, which is how a calibration ends
  up with a rare ore that is present everywhere and dominant nowhere.
- **Nothing stated how much of a typical map each mineral should occupy.**
  "Rarity labels are targets for calibration" was the whole requirement, so
  "mostly stone and quartz without other" was a legitimate outcome of the plan.

Measured with the plan's design ranges and its host weight of 0.08: Stone took
61.8% of the field, 55.2% of cells were over 90% host, native silver 0.13% and
gold 0.37%. Exactly the failure to fix.

## Shipped

- `js/minerals.js`
  - The nine-row table of the plan: id, name, symbol, analogue, per-channel
    `[low, high]` ranges, rarity tier, strength in J/cell, collapse growth,
    colour and demand period/depth. `host: true` marks Stone, whose mix weight
    is the constant host weight and never a range.
  - The calibration targets themselves: rarity weights (common 1.00, uncommon
    0.45, rare 0.19, very rare 0.07), the target host share (0.32) and the
    derived `rarityTargets` array, so the lab, the probe and the tests tune
    against one set of numbers.
  - Ranges are the calibrated ones (§2 of the plan), not the design ones.
- `js/element-cdf.js` — the measured cumulative distribution of the value-noise
  field on 257 nodes, generated by `experiments/element-cdf.cjs` from
  `js/geology.js` itself (no second copy of the noise). `experiments/geology.test.js`
  fails if it goes stale.
- `js/geology.js` — the element field, the mineral mix and the calibration,
  shared by the page, the lab and the probes:
  - `createField({seed, frequency, yCompression})` samples three independent
    channels, each flattened through the CDF table, with the 3× geological Y
    compression applied to all three.
  - `membership()` / `featherFor()`: 1 inside a range, falling to 0 across
    `min(0.08, width * 0.35)` outside it.
  - `compile(ranges, mode, specificity)` bakes memberships into a 1024-node
    lookup per mineral and channel (within 0.005 of the exact curve) and builds
    `affinityInto` / `mixInto`. `balanced` takes the geometric mean over the
    constrained channels; `product` keeps the plain product for comparison.
    Specificity multiplies an affinity by `1 + 0.6 * (1 - meanConstrainedWidth)`.
  - `buildCache` / `statsAt` / `meanHostShare` / `solveHostWeight`: per-cell ore
    affinities independent of the host weight, so shares are an analytic
    function of it and the host weight can be bisected against a target share.
    `statsAt` reports share, dominant share, visible share (≥ 25% of a cell),
    and how much of the field is over 90% host or over 90% one ore.
  - `separate(ranges, escape)` moves a contained signature out of the box that
    contains it by the smallest shift that leaves it sticking out.
  - `autoAdjust()` alternates width scaling (`(target / measured)^0.3`, clamped
    to 0.8…1.25 per pass) with the spread pass and re-solves the host weight,
    with a 0.06 floor so an ore cell keeps some matrix.
- `prototypes/mineral-lab.html`, `prototypes/mineral-lab.css`,
  `prototypes/mineral-lab.js`, `prototypes/range-slider.js`
  - The rock as one pixel per cell in four views (blended mineral, dominant,
    element cube, host share) with a hover inspector, plus the three element
    channels as separate previews.
  - Seed, field frequency, Y flattening, map depth, sample seeds and sample
    depth; the map redraws and the numbers re-measure as they move.
  - A two-handle range slider component with the channel histogram and the
    mineral's yield curve drawn behind the track, pointer/touch/keyboard
    driven, dragging live and measuring on release.
  - Auto-adjust, spread, reset, copy-JS-ranges and copy-report, and the rarity
    weights as sliders.
- `experiments/mineral-field-probe.cjs`, `experiments/geology.test.js`,
  `experiments/logs/minerals.2-field.txt`.

The two ores that moved furthest are the two that traded chemical tidiness for
being findable. Gold reads as a high-metal, volatile-rich vein rather than
"metal with a little quartz" — defensible for hydrothermal gold, but it is the
first thing to revisit if the identity matters more than the share.

## Validation

- `node --test experiments/*.test.js`: 256 tests, 256 pass (13 of them new, in
  `experiments/geology.test.js`: table shape, element evenness per decile, the
  remap versus the raw bell shape, the 3× seam ratio, bounded and monotone
  membership, the lookup table against the exact curve, normalised mixes with a
  pure-host fallback, every ore leading part of the element cube, the
  balanced-versus-product comparison, the host weight solver, calibration
  convergence, and the shipped table's share/dominant/patch floors).
- `node experiments/mineral-field-probe.cjs --seeds=5 --columns=120 --depth=240`:
  element deciles 8.1–10.9%, seam ratio 2.89/3.10/2.95, host 32.0% against a
  32.0% target, every ore visible in ≥ 2.25% of cells. Log kept in
  `experiments/logs/minerals.2-field.txt`.
- The lab page was exercised headlessly (jsdom, canvas stubbed): it boots with
  no errors, builds nine share rows and three two-handle sliders, drags a
  handle, runs auto-adjust (5 passes, 2.3% worst error) and produces its
  report. It still needs a real browser pass by the user for the visuals.
