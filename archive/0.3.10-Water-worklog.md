# The water gun: fluid solver, chemistry ledger, and the jet that would not cut

## Previous-task review

`js/water.js` arrived with a solver that conserved water perfectly and dissolved
nothing. Every yield probe reported `0` cells and `0.00 g` dissolved while the
droplets were visibly leaving the nozzle, hitting the rock, and running back
down. The first instinct - "the chemistry is wrong" - was wrong. Three separate
faults sat between the muzzle and the rock face, and each one alone was enough
to make the gun look inert. Finding them meant building a trace instead of
reading the code again.

## Fault 1: the face search could not look down

`faceNear` was called with a single point, the droplet's position at the end of
the step, and it searched rock rows from `centreRow` clamped to `-2..2`. A jet
rising from a nozzle at `0.84 m` meets faces that live *above* it, so the row
index it needed was negative relative to a point that had already reached the
face. The clamp was harmless; the single-point test was the bug. A droplet
moving `4.8 m/s` covers `0.077 m` in one `16 ms` step - most of a rock cell - so
the endpoint can sit inside the open parcel *under* a face while the face itself
was never sampled.

The fix samples the whole step at one rock-cell spacing and derives the reach
from the view: `Math.ceil(cellSize / rockView().cell)`, which is 4 at a `0.25 m`
parcel and a `0.125 m` cell. Literal reaches of 3 and 2 were tried first and
were both too short, which is why the reach is now computed rather than typed.

## Fault 2: a droplet that stopped under a face was counted as spilled

`stepDroplets` treated a droplet as spent when it left the chamber or landed in
a solid parcel. A droplet whose last step ended in the open parcel beneath a
face did neither: it was recorded as splashed water and the face was never
struck. The stop test now includes `face >= 0`, so an impact on a found face is
a strike rather than a miss.

## Fault 3: the aim was measured from the platform, not the nozzle

`nozzleOf` took the machine and used its centre; the barrel sits at
`collectorFraction 0.35` of a `3.6 m` platform, so the muzzle of a rig parked at
`x = 12` is at `10.83`, not `12`. Aiming at the platform's own column therefore
threw the jet almost a metre off the column the player was looking at. The
resolver now returns the collector's x and `fire` aims from it.

## The fourth suspicion was not a bug

Once the trace showed `aheadFace` returning cell `83` and `strikeCell(83, ...)`
running, nothing dissolved - and an isolated call to
`evaluateTableSplit` reported `speed 0.000`. That looked like a broken law. It
was not: cell `83` is 48% stone and 49% of a `dissolveSpeed 0` mineral, so its
split genuinely has no speed. A hand-check of the mixture instead of a single
spot sample showed the arithmetic was right. The lesson is recorded here because
it cost the most time: **a zero on one cell is a composition, not a formula
bug.** Sweeping the bed settled it - 1613 of 5568 cells (29.0%) are chemically
active at all, so a gun aimed at an arbitrary column is aimed at inert rock three
times out of four.

Two suspected bugs were cleared the same way and did not need a fix:

- `dissolvedMass > 0` with `dissolvedCells == 0` is legitimate. The mass total
  also counts submerged grain dissolution, which leaches material without a cell
  ever leaving the rock.
- `maxSaturation` sitting at `0.000` is legitimate. The cap is `60 g` of load per
  unit of water and a cell weighs `0.6 g`, so the parcels stay far from
  saturation in a 30 s probe, and a stagnant pool never gets the chance to stall.

## After the fix

`node experiments/water-yield-probe.cjs` (`experiments/logs/0.3.10-water-yield.txt`),
42 sprayed samples over six seeds and seven positions, 30 s each:

| | p25 | median | p75 | mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cells dissolved | 0 | 0 | 4 | 3.2 |
| caught (cr) | 0.0 | 0.5 | 6.6 | - |
| profit (cr) | -497.8 | -497.7 | -496.2 | -958.9 |

Dissolved `1.03 g`, vented `0.21 g`, residue `0.59 g`, energy `953.8 cr` at the
paid import rate. The `no spray` control row is zero across the board, so the
jet is doing all of the work - the mean is up from `1.7` to `3.2` cells and from
`0.63 g` to `1.03 g` against the pre-fix run. A single column measured by hand
(`x = 15`, nuclear pack, 30 s) dissolves 7 cells for `2.184 g`, which is the same
rate seen from the other end.

The gun dissolves rock but it does not yet *earn*: `-497 cr` median is the
energy bill, and the hopper only catches `0.5 cr` of it. That is an honest
place for the tool to sit while the balance question is still open - the
sandblaster was measured the same way and reported the same shape of result.

## Behaviour changed on purpose

The pump order is now a chamber setting. `settings.pumpOn` was reset to `true`
in `reset()` while the checkbox is a player's standing order, so unchecking the
pump and starting a new game left the box clear and the pump running. The other
chamber rules (`GROUND`, `PERSPECTIVE`, the shape rule) all survive New game, and
the pump now does too; the readout already re-syncs the checkbox from module
state, so the two cannot disagree. A comment on `setModel` claimed the chemistry
model persisted across runs. It does not - `reset()` puts it back to `table` -
and the comment now says what the code does. `experiments/water.test.js` was
updated to assert the new pump rule.

## Follow-up: a rival jet crashed the page

The merged build threw `Cannot read properties of undefined (reading 'id')` on
every frame once a rival fired. The cause was the aim fix above: `fire` resolved
the owner's machine and then handed *that machine* to `nozzleOf`, which expects
the **world** - `machineById(world, id)` and `collectorX(world, owner)` both
resolve the owner's own body themselves. It survived testing because the rig's
machine handle *is* the world, so only a rival - a bare body with no `platform`
field - reached the bad call. The fix is one line: pass `target`, not `machine`,
and name the parameter `world` so the next reader cannot make the same mistake.

The lesson is about the test suite, not the code: every water test ran on a rig
with `players.init(world, 0)`, so no rival existed in any of them. The new test
`a rival fires from its own collector, not the rig's` builds a roster with a
competitor and asserts the nozzle lands on *its* collector (17.83 when the rival
stands at 19), that the rig's reserve is untouched, and that the throw is booked
as imported work on the chamber.

One detour is worth recording because it looked like a second bug and was not:
`rival.importedEnergy` is `NaN`, and it should be - a rival body carries no such
field. Rival spending is booked on `world.importedEnergy` by the same convention
the sandblaster uses, and `fire`'s `target` parameter *is* the world. The test now
asserts the chamber ledger, which is the real contract.

Validation: `node --test experiments/water.test.js` 11/11 (the rival case added),
`players.test.js` 45/45, `autopilot.test.js` 0 failures, interface water case
passing.

## Next step

The tool works and is honest; the balance is not. The next shift should raise the
yield rather than move the price: `jetDissolvePower` (1.9) and `jetWashSeconds`
(0.06) set how much of a cell one hit takes, and the probe shows a cell needs
about six hits at the current face - the cheapest change is to let a hit wash
longer, or to widen the footprint so a pool keeps working a face for a while
after the jet has moved on. Re-run the probe after each change and read the
`cells` median rather than the mean, which is dominated by the columns that
happen to land on reactive rock. Only then decide whether `shotEnergy` (135 J)
should come down; the economics are a separate lever from the yield, and moving
both at once makes the result unreadable.
