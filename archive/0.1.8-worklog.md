# 0.1.8 — Technology tree, ballistic computer, and a closable energy economy

## Requested tuning

- Telemetry numerals 1.5× smaller: 25 px → 17 px, and 20 px → 13 px in the narrow layout. The strip moved out of the full-page row into the chamber panel directly above the canvas, so it spans exactly the canvas width and keeps its four-column grid.
- Platform engine force 1.5× bigger: 270 N → 400 N (405 rounded down to a whole number).
- Platform max speed 1.5× bigger: 7 m/s → 10 m/s (10.5 rounded down).

## Review of the previous step

- `movePlatform` sold cargo at a wall clamp and `step()` sold again at the wall every step. Removed the duplicate; `step()` also covers fragments caught during the same step.
- Equipment owned the "installed mass couples to the moving platform" rule inline. Moved to `SteamPhysics.applyMassChange`, now shared by equipment and research instead of being written twice.
- Dead style rules removed: `.equipment-row .installed` (no element ever carried the class) and a `max-width: 1100px` telemetry rule that repeated the base grid.
- Stale copy fixed: the drive hint promised "at most 36 J" while the pulse is a live value, so it now points at the installed specification.

## Decisions

- The stronger drive tripled the scripted policy's average draw (77 W → 219 W), which drains the 12 kJ reserve in about a minute and previously dead-ended the game: no force, no pulse, no way to earn energy back. Reserve recharge was added so the mine–collect–sell–upgrade loop closes without developer controls (delivery gate 4). Energy is imported at 25 cr/kJ up to capacity, buying only what current funds cover; `world.importedEnergy` is subtracted in `totalEnergy`, mirroring exported (sold, cooled) energy, so the balance stays exact in both trade directions.
- Technology tree: six nodes with explicit prerequisites and locked / available / researched states. Every node changes a measured quantity (retention, dry mass, pulse, convective cooling) or unlocks a real component (brake flywheel, ballistic computer), so none is a label-only unlock. Equipment items name the node that unlocks them; a refused purchase states whether the reason is funds, a missing prerequisite, or a duplicate.
- Brake flywheel: braking energy is returned to the reserve at 55% × 90% round-trip efficiency, the rest stays as heat. Wall stops remain pure dissipation — a crash is not braking — so recovery cannot be farmed by ramming walls.
- Ballistic computer: prediction is passive (no drive pulses) and terminates at the first rock, deck, or ground contact or at the 3 s horizon. Rock contacts end the path flagged uncertain rather than guessing a rebound through damaged rock. Rock contacts snap to the trace's separated contact point, which is where the simulation snaps the ball, so predicted and simulated contacts agree. Intercept feasibility is a thrust bound (ramp at the current force setting, then cruise) and is reported as reachable or as the missing distance; the ghost coasts at constant platform velocity.
- One installed-specification readout (dry mass, engine force, top speed, pulse, retention, convective cooling, brake recovery) is the "installed versus available" comparison: it shows the live result of every delta next to the rows that state what buying changes.
- Rejected: a cached scan bound over the debris pool. The typed arrays are public for tests and tools that mark slots active externally, so a cached bound could silently hide fragments from the simulation, the energy term, and the renderer. Measured cost is about 11 µs per step (0.26% of one core at 240 Hz), which does not justify the fragility.

## Implementation

- `js/research.js` (new): frozen node table with cost, prerequisites, delta channels and an optional capability; `status`/`isResearched` reads, funds- and prerequisite-gated `research(id, world)`, `reset()`, and a stable live `state` view with property getters.
- `js/prediction.js` (new): fixed-horizon passive march into preallocated `Float64Array` buffers, reusing the simulation's wall/ceiling/ground/deck event maths and `SteamCeiling.trace` for rock; exposes samples, kind, endpoint, time, uncertainty, ghost position, reachability and gap. Read-only and allocation-free per call.
- `js/physics.js`: 400 N / 10 m/s drive response, `recoveryEfficiency`, one `installed(channel)` aggregator over equipment and research deltas, `applyMassChange`, `dissipate` (flywheel recovery on braking), `conductanceNow`, `importedEnergy` in the world and in `totalEnergy`, duplicate wall sale removed, and `retentionNow`/`pulseNow`/`recoveryNow`/`conductanceNow`/`radiatorArea` exported for the overlay and the specification readout.
- `js/equipment.js`: `requires` per item, new brake-flywheel item, `recoveryFraction` channel, `isUnlocked`, installation delegating mass coupling to physics.
- `js/ceiling.js`: `purchase(label, amount, time, type)` so research and fuel entries keep their own ledger type; `recharge(world, time)` and `rechargeCost(world)`; `fuelPrice` in the data table and the live state.
- `js/main.js`, `index.html`, `styles.css`: telemetry strip inside the chamber panel above the canvas; technology panel in the mining row (three columns now); shared `upgrade-row` builder for equipment and research with locked/available/researched styling; installed-specification list; recharge control in the market panel; ledger and status wording for research and fuel entries; ballistic overlay drawn as a dotted path with a landing ring and intercept ghost, plus a status line that names the landing, the ghost, and the reachability margin.
- `experiments/harness.cjs` (new): shared funds/measurement helpers so equipment, research, prediction and economy tests fund purchases from real cargo sales.
- `experiments/dom.cjs` (new): minimal DOM shim built from the real `index.html`, with a recording canvas context and manually driven frames.
- `experiments/research.test.js`, `experiments/prediction.test.js`, `experiments/economy.test.js`, `experiments/interface.test.js` (new); `experiments/equipment.test.js` rewritten for research gating and the flywheel; `experiments/physics.test.js` updated for the new drive response; `experiments/mine-trips.cjs` extended with a progression policy (recharge first, then upgrades) and gross-income reporting; `experiments/browser-smoke.cjs` extended for the telemetry placement, new panels and gating messages.
- `README.md` and `0.1.0-plan.md` updated: new calibration numbers, confirmed progression/economy rules, and the next design decision.

## Validation

- `node --test experiments/*.test.js`: 56/56 pass — ceiling 11, physics 20, equipment 5, research 3, prediction 7, economy 3, interface 7.
- Prediction accuracy: predicted ground and wall-reflected landings match the simulation's contact within one fixed step in time and 1e-6 m in position; deck contacts match to 1e-9 s; rock contacts end at the same separated point the simulation snaps to. Prediction leaves the world byte-identical (`JSON.stringify` comparison) and the rock untouched, and repeats identically.
- Interface run (no browser needed): the real page scripts boot against the shim, build 5 equipment rows, 6 research rows and 14 specification entries, refuse locked and unaffordable upgrades with reasons, toggle the drive with Space/Q after a Pause click, research and install from measured sales, show the overlay only after the ballistic computer, recharge the reserve, and reset to a clean state with the balance error under 1e-5 J.
- Economy: recharge imports exactly the priced energy, never overspends funds, buys partially on a short budget, refuses when full or unaffordable, and keeps the balance exact; an empty reserve provably stops the platform until credits buy energy back.
- `experiments/mine-trips.cjs` (8 seeds per rock shape, 240 s cap, log at `experiments/logs/0.1.8-mine-trips.txt`): base tuning grosses 559 cr/shift smooth (614 cr/min) and 334 cr/shift square, at 219 W and 61 W average draw, mean peak temperature 98.6 °C and 99.9 °C, mean minimum drive output 73% and 70%. With the progression policy a shift earns 550 cr smooth and 620 cr square, spends 446 cr and 511 cr, owns 1.4 and 1.8 of 11 upgrades, and recharges 0.8 and 0.6 times importing 7.0 kJ and 5.9 kJ. These are calibration measurements, not a final balance claim.
- Performance: `physics.step` 10.8 µs (2.6 ms per simulated second at 240 Hz); `prediction.predict` 4.4 µs for a low flight, 15.5 µs near intact rock, 34.9 µs over a carved crater, recomputed at 10 Hz.
- `node --check` on every application and experiment script; `git diff --check`: clean. Browser smoke updated but not run — Playwright and a browser binary are still unavailable in this environment.

## Next step

Decide the next energy era and its resupply model before further economy tuning: at 219 W the reserve is a per-minute consumable, so coal/steam refuelling (fuel mass, burn rate, pollution, crew consequences) or electric storage (capacity, charge rate, mass) changes what a shift costs. Then build autonomous interception on the existing prediction (it already reports the reachability margin), add wear from load, impacts, braking and temperature, and consider a drive-aware prediction variant so the overlay can show the effect of powered bounces instead of only passive flight.
