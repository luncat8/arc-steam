# 0.3.5 worklog — rockets and automatic purchase

## Review of minerals.4

The palette, the absolute rubble limit and the sand rows were read end to end
and hold together: `colorFor`, the inspector's state tag and `collapseEligible`
all treat 0.8 J as "not rubble" with the same strict comparison, so gas rock at
exactly the limit is sound in the picture, the tooltip and the scheduler.

One defect in the filler's price. `demandMean` multiplied only the live quote,
so the base table still priced sand by its abundance alone:

- the market board showed sand at 0.7–1.1 cr against a 26–30 cr base, a
  permanent −97% "drift" that no market move could ever close;
- the base table was normalised as if those 26–30 cr existed, so the reported
  mean cell (9.9–12.5 cr on six seeds) included about 1.1 cr that was never
  paid. The realised mean cell was 9.4–11.7 cr against the 12.5 cr target.

`demandMean` is now a factor of the base quote and sits inside the
normalisation in `materials.basePricesInto`; the market's seed bias swings
around that base and no longer carries the factor. Sand is based at
0.6–1.3 cr and quotes 0.8–1.6 cr, and the realised mean cell is 10.1–12.6 cr.
The remaining gap to the target is the 150 cr cap on the rarest ores, which
`basePriceReport` already reports.

Smaller leftovers removed:

- `materials.minStrength` had no reader left but one test; the test now asks
  the table directly whether a material forms below the limit.
- A duplicate `setDepthScale` key in the ceiling's `api` object.
- The floor-compression block in `js/ceiling.js`, `spend` in `js/physics.js`
  and the tail of the physics `api` object were not indented with the module.
- The `deposit` comment still described a pile capped below the deck and a
  fixed 4:1 tile.

The previous step asked for a browser look at the Remaining strength view.
Chromium runs in this sandbox with the recipe in `findings-pitfalls-skills.md`,
so it was checked on a rocket-chipped bed: the weakened patch darkens along
the ramp, the released cells fall as grey crumbs, and the warm/grey boundary
is readable at the default cell size.

## Plan

The plan was rewritten as the final rule set and moved to
`archive/0.3.5-plan.md`. Decisions it now states that the draft left open:

- A "shop visit" is contact with either `SELL` wall, the place the rig already
  trades. A visit lasts while the contact does, so a sale during the visit can
  finish the batch and no visit buys a fourth rocket.
- A 6-rocket rack bounds an unattended standing order.
- The launch tube is on the collector, which is what makes placement pay: the
  bin sits under the rock its own rocket loosened.
- The ±20% is on the fuse, cut for the gap to the rock face. A long fuse is a
  contact burst, a short one an open burst.
- Fragments use the ball's blow rule instead of a second damage model, with
  their own energy and a 1–4 cell radius. They release nothing; the loosened
  rock falls through the collapse scheduler, which is what "fall as normal
  debris" has to mean for material to stay conserved.
- Rockets carry their own energy, outside the rig's ledger, like a
  competitor's ball.

## Implementation

- `js/ceiling.js`: the body of `damage` became `weaken(x, y, intensity, gain,
  radius)`. `damage` passes the owner's multiplier and radius and is unchanged
  in result; `chip(x, y, impactJ, radius)` is the fragment's entry.
  `surfaceAt(x)` returns the free face in the current rock shape.
- `js/physics.js`: `atSellWall(world)` replaces the inline wall test of the
  sale; the step advances the rockets after the sale; `createWorld` resets them.
- `js/rockets.js` (new, `SteamRockets`): rack and purchases, the visit rule,
  typed-array pools for 8 rockets and 192 shards, a seeded stream, the step and
  the draw pass. No allocation after load.
- Page: ROCKETS block in the controls panel (`rocket-fire`, `rocket-buy`,
  `rocket-auto`, `rocket-status`), the `R` key, a launch tube drawn on the
  rig's collector, the flight pass after the balls, a `rockets` help topic and
  an `R` row in the key list. Test mode fills the rack as a grant.

Found while building:

- With seed 1 fed raw into the generator, the first rocket of every run was a
  dud. The seed is scrambled before the first draw; the first dud of the
  shipped seed is now rocket 22.
- The reload countdown kept a 1e-17 remainder and held the tube for one step
  too long. It is counted to the nearest step.
- A burst on the ground loses the half of its fan thrown downward at once.
  Kept as physics; the test states it.
- The first yield probe was contaminated: at some positions the parked rig
  picked up the grounded ball and the bounce drive mined with it. The probe now
  switches the drive off and prints a no-rocket control row, which reads zero.

## Calibration

`node experiments/rocket-yield-probe.cjs`, log in
`experiments/logs/0.3.5-rocket-yield.txt`. One rocket, fresh bed, rig parked,
30 s, 21 positions × 6 seeds:

- cells brought down: median 3, mean 4.2; 2.6 of them within the first 5 s;
- 10.5 of the 24 shards reach the rock;
- value brought down: median 17.1 cr, mean 121 cr;
- caught by the parked bin: median 14.3 cr, mean 92 cr;
- the same catch at ordinary-rock prices: median 11.9 cr, mean 14.5 cr.

The price is 14 cr: zero expected profit in ordinary rock. The mean of the real
catch is 6.5 times that because weak minerals fall first and the weak minerals
include the dearest. Whether that tail is too generous belongs to the balance
plan (0.3.8), which now has a probe to measure it with.

Cost: one chip is about 37 µs (heightmap and region rescan included), so a
whole fan landing in one step costs under 1 ms.
