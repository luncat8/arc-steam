# 0.4.0 Wild Mine — M3, the ball player (D1–D5)

## 1. M2 review

Reading M2 back against the M3 brief turned up four things that had to change
before the ball could move at all:

- **`ballEnergy` knew only half a ball.** The classic body is translation plus
  height; a Wild Mine ball also carries `0.2 m r² ω²` and its own battery. Without
  those two terms `physics.totalEnergy` cannot close invariant 5. `ballEnergy`
  now adds them behind the wild guard, and `createWorld` seeds the battery, so a
  classic run is bit-for-bit unchanged.
- **`canLaunch` was hard-locked in Wild Mine.** M2 returned `false` for the whole
  mode; it now asks the ball module, which in turn asks the machine it is held by
  (a release needs that platform's drive on), and the classic rule is untouched.
- **The held pose was re-seated by `wild-mine.step`.** A module that owns the
  held, deck and flying states must be the only writer of the pose, otherwise the
  seat and the step disagree every frame. The re-seat moved into
  `SteamWildBall.step`; `wild-mine.js` now only calls `init`/`step`.
- **No ball panel.** The chamber's own `#ball-controls` row did not exist, so D1
  had neither a button nor a key. It is added hidden in Classic and shown by the
  wild readout; the arrows are ignored unless Wild Mine is active, and the
  `#status-ball` line keeps its `Wild Mine ·` prefix (the M2 interface test
  asserts it).

## 2. The ball (`js/wild-ball.js`)

One module owns the whole body. `settleRoll`, `bookLift` and `bookMech` are the
only writers of the ball's share of the ledger, so every mechanical change is
either booked to the rock, to the battery, to heat, or as imported work.

### Step order

1. Debris is advanced (it is outside the ball's ledger).
2. A held ball is seated on the piston and inductively charged, then stops.
3. A ball that was in rock contact last step and still sits within
   `contactSlop` of the surface takes `rollStep`; anything else takes `airStep`.
4. `deckStep` probes the platform capsule; a deck contact can bounce, spin the
   ball at fixed energy, charge it, and capture it.
5. A rock contact that has left the surface is dropped to `CONTACT_NONE`.

`rollStep` holds the ball on the surface (the normal part of the step's motion is
absorbed, booked as heat), applies the queued jump, gravity's tangential
component, the motor torque and rolling resistance, then moves on a trapezoid so
the gravity work matches the potential change exactly, and pushes out of any SDF
overlap.

### Contact classification (D5)

`resolveContact` is one function for both a traced contact and a contact that is
already touching at the start of the step:

- `v_n < −bounceFloor` **or** the normal turning more than `curvatureLimit`
  (`30°`) across one ball diameter → **hit**: `e = sqrt(0.9)`,
  `E = ½ m v_n² (1 − e²)` is the fracture budget handed to `cave.weaken` at
  `ball − n·r`, the removed strength is `world.fractureEnergy` and the rest is
  heat, then `spawnRemoved` turns each opened cell into a fragment.
- otherwise → **roll**: the normal motion is absorbed as heat, no fracture, and
  `settleRoll` re-solves `v_t = ω r` at fixed energy.

An already-overlapping ball is separated first, the push's height change is
booked as imported work, and the contact is then resolved as a hit or a roll at
the separated centre. It is never silently absorbed: a 6 m/s drop that starts on
the surface must still punch rock.

### Battery (D1, D2)

`batteryCapacity = 300 J`, induction `60 W` at 85% while the ball is held or on
the deck (`phys().spend` takes it from the platform reserve, the waste is heat).
The motor accelerates along the contact tangent at `3.2 m/s²` to `5 m/s` and
prices the exact tangential kinetic energy it buys at 80%; the jump adds
`2.6 m/s` along the contact normal (preserving `v_t` and `ω`) at the same
efficiency; the release kick is `3.2 m/s` along the reflector face. A deck
contact with `releaseCooldown <= 0` captures the ball back into the collector by
merging its momentum with the platform's, so a run ends with a recharge.

### Release geometry (D1)

The shot starts at the reflector's own station — platform-local
`(pistonCentre, deck + radius)` rotated by the plate angle — so every tilt
releases from above the hull instead of inside it. The plate's own step velocity
is the ball's initial velocity, and the tilt is what aims the kick.

### Debris (invariant 7)

One fragment per opened cell, typed arrays (`maxDebris = 512`, oldest recycled),
colour from `cave.mixAt` at spawn. A fragment falls under real gravity, is
separated along the SDF normal, bounces with `0.2` restitution and friction, and
rests at `half its cell` off the face — its drawn square sits in the hole it
came out of. A face steeper than `debrisSteep` keeps sliding instead of resting.

## 3. Defects found while verifying

Four real defects, each found by a node probe before it reached a test:

1. **A deck contact minted rotation.** The first `deckStep` set
   `ω = v_t / r`, which is energy for free (the drill read `+1.19 J`). Replaced
   with `settleRoll`, which splits the pair's existing energy.
2. **A jump double-booked its impulse.** `rollStep` captured `startVy` after the
   impulse and then integrated the whole step from it, so the lift was counted
   twice (`5.3e−2`, later `1.19`). The impulse now fires before the capture and
   returns, so the next step is a plain projectile that books its own gravity.
3. **An already-touching contact swallowed a fast drop.** `traceCircle` reports
   `time = 0` both for "overlapping" and for "exactly on the surface, moving in".
   The overlap branch treated every `time = 0` contact as a push, so a ball set
   down at `surface + radius` with `v_y = −6` booked 5.8 J as heat and never
   counted a hit (`hits 0`, no debris). Both cases now go through
   `resolveContact`.
4. **The first overlap branch pumped the normal speed.** Flying a full step from
   the pushed centre without killing the inward normal component let a crevice
   narrower than the ball accelerate it to `v_y = −38 m/s` in a few dozen steps.
   The inward normal is now stopped and booked as heat before the flight, which
   is the same rule the roll path uses.

`node --test experiments/*.test.js` → **477 / 477 pass** (468 before M3, plus
eight new ball tests in `experiments/wild-mine.test.js` and one interface test).

Measured samples (seed 1, start shelf floor `30.875 m`, ball `r = 0.18`,
`m = 3.24`):

| Probe | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Ball set down on the floor (`4 mm`, `−0.2 m/s`) | rolls, `y 31.0550` = floor + r, `CONTACT_ROCK`, drift `0` |
| Motor `0.5 s` from rest | 3.36 J drawn, `v_x 1.026`, `ω 5.70 = v/r`, drift `0` |
| Jump from rest | `v_y 2.57`, impulse energy exact, drift `0` |
| Drop `v_y −6` | 1 hit, `5.832 J` fracture, `v_y 5.69` after, drift `0` |
| Wall hit at `6 m/s` | 1 hit, `5.832 J`, `v_x −5.69`, drift `5.8e−11` |

| Deck home | caught at `2.11 s`, battery back to `300 J`, drift `0` |
| Tilted launch (`−60°`) with the motor on, 12 s | 2 hits, caught back on the deck, drift `0` (max `5.8e−11`) |
| 40 drops at `−8 m/s` | 45 fragments, all resting at `sdfAt = half cell`, none in rock |

`experiments/wild-mine-interface.test.js` covers the panel's visibility per mode,
`launch`, `←`/`→` motor keys and buttons, `↑` jump queueing, and that the arrows
do nothing to a classic ball.

The browser scripts could not run in this sandbox (`playwright` and a browser
binary are not installed), so the Chromium smoke and layout passes from M2 were
not repeated here; the DOM shim checks (`experiments/dom.cjs` through the
interface tests) still police script order, duplicate globals and element ids.

## 5. Known limits

- A crevice narrower than the ball is not a physical contact. The push out of it
  is booked as imported work, so the ledger stays exact but the ball can creep a
  few centimetres up a wedge over tens of seconds instead of jamming. The
  observed total over 40 s in the start room's eroded rim was `1.4e−2 J` of
  imported work.
- A ball dropped on rock bounces until its normal speed falls under
  `bounceFloor`; at `e = sqrt(0.9)` and the chamber's slow ball gravity
  (`3.03 m/s²`) that is about a dozen bounces over some ten seconds. It settles
  and the ledger is exact, but the tail feels long: if M4 tunes feel rather than
  rules, the candidates are a floor on the *rebound* speed or a slightly lower
  restitution.
- `cave.weaken`'s `hitLooseCap` lets a blow take at most half a cell's remaining
  strength, so a cell only opens after roughly fifteen hits on the same spot
  (measured: the first fragment at drop 14 of 40). That is the chamber's own
  rule, kept for comparability; it makes the ball a slow drill and belongs with
  M6's collapse pass if it needs loosening.

## 6. Next step

**M4 — streaming under motion.** The pieces that exist already stream on camera
movement (`wild-mine.step`), but nothing has measured it at speed: drive the rig
across a chunk seam at `12 m/s` and assert that (a) no frame generates more than
one chunk, (b) an edited chunk evicted and re-entered holds its edits, and (c) the
SDF queries at the seam match a freshly built chunk. The ball makes the seam case
load-bearing because it can now leave the platform and travel on its own, so the
stream window has to follow the ball as well as the camera.
