# 0.2.1 — Tilting reflector piston, drive on by default, change-only panel writes

Sources: the user's three requests and the `draft.txt` "platform's piston" notes.

## Review of the previous step

- The 0.2.0 warning state already wrote DOM only on change (`setWarning`), but the rest of the 10 Hz readout did not: upgrade buttons, row states, drive/launch state, status lines, the installed specification, inventory, inspector and refuel controls were all reassigned every readout even when the new value was identical to the old one.
- The reflector was a flat strip drawn on the deck: reflections and powered pushes could only leave vertically, so the only steering was platform position and horizontal friction.
- The bounce drive started off, so a fresh world (and every reset) needed a toggle before a held ball could be released or a bounce could gain energy.

## Decisions

- The reflector is the platform's **piston**: a plate spanning the reflector zone (centre at platform + 0.63 m, half-width 1.17 m, following the collector/reflector split). Its face tilts from −10° to +10°; a positive angle raises the right edge and sends a falling ball left, negative sends it right.
- Tilt is real physics, not a render effect. Contacts are solved on the tilted plane with the platform's step velocity included (`pistonContactTime`, also exported so the ballistic computer predicts deck contacts on the same plane), friction acts along the face, the passive reflection follows the face normal with the platform's horizontal reaction booked, and a powered push adds its energy along the normal. The exact kinetic-energy difference of a piston contact is booked as heat, so the ledger stays exact at any tilt.
- Animation is rendered state only: a contact sets `pistonOffset` in proportion to the normal impact speed (0.024 m per m/s, capped at the 0.16 m travel) and a damped spring (650, damping 18) in `advancePiston` extends it again, overshooting slightly as the visible push before settling in about half a second. The collision plane stays at the rest position, so the animation can never feed back into the simulation. Release also compresses the piston, so launching reads as a shove.
- Three inputs share one writer (`setPistonAngle` in `js/main.js`): the `PISTON TILT` slider (−10…+10° in 0.5° steps), the mouse wheel over the chamber (about 2° per notch, page scroll prevented), and a drag through open air — the zone that previously did nothing, so rock inspection and low-band travel clicks keep their exact pointer-down behaviour. Dragging right or up raises the angle. The slider and readout only move when the angle changed.
- The drive is enabled by default: `createWorld` starts with `drive: true`, the markup ships `aria-pressed="true"` / "Drive on", and reset therefore restores an enabled drive. Tests that measure a *passive* bounce now switch the drive off explicitly.
- Readout writes are change-only outside telemetry. `setText` / `setDisabled` / `setHidden` guard text, disabled and hidden; `setBlocked`, the drive `aria-pressed`, row `dataset.state`, the strength meter, the force and piston sliders are likewise only assigned on a real change. Telemetry numbers (clock, reserve, energies, temperature, motion) still write directly every readout, as requested. One-off writes on real events (button clicks, ledger rebuilds after a new entry) are unchanged.

## Implementation

- `js/physics.js`: piston tuning in config, `world.pistonAngle/pistonOffset/pistonVelocity/pistonImpact`, `setPistonAngle` (±10° clamp), `pistonContactTime`, `advancePiston`, piston contact as event type 6 (the flat deck event is now collector-only capture), tilted launch resting height, powered push along the normal, `drive: true` by default.
- `js/prediction.js`: collector contacts stay on the flat plane; piston contacts use `physics.pistonContactTime` at the world's current tilt.
- `js/main.js`: change-only writers throughout `readout`, `miningReadout`, `refreshUpgrades`, `specReadout` and `predictionReadout`; piston single writer plus slider sync; wheel and air-drag listeners; the piston plate, rod and compression drawn in the chamber.
- `index.html`: drive on in markup, piston slider with hint, recovery wording, reflector-piston legend.
- `styles.css`: no changes needed; the existing range styling covers the piston slider.
- `experiments/physics.test.js`: default-on drive and ±10° clamp; positive/negative tilt steering with exact balance; compression scaling with impact speed and spring recovery; powered push along the tilted normal.
- `experiments/interface.test.js`: boot and shortcut expectations for the default-on drive; a write-counting test that freezes the world and proves settled readouts perform zero text/disabled/attribute writes on buttons, status and controls; slider/wheel/air-drag piston input with clamping.
- `experiments/equipment.test.js`, `experiments/research.test.js`: passive retention measurements switch the (now default-on) drive off.
- `experiments/browser-smoke.cjs`: default-on drive sequence, piston slider and chamber-wheel assertions, reset levels the piston.
- `README.md`, `0.1.0-plan.md`: piston rules, controls, and the change-only readout rule.

## Validation

- `node --test experiments/*.test.js`: 67/67 pass.
- Tilted long-run check (scripted chase, ±10°/±5°/0°, 30 s each, drive on): energy-balance error 0 at every tilt; predicted tilted deck contacts match the simulated bounce in time and position exactly.
- Edge sweep (5 tilts × 21 contact positions × 3 impact speeds): every drop bounces once, no surface penetration, exact balance.
- Headless Chromium smoke (`playwright-core` + `@sparticuz/chromium`): PASS, no page errors; real mouse wheel and air drag verified against the slider value.
- Full-page screenshots at 1440 and 390 px reviewed: the tilted piston plate and rod are readable in the chamber, the controls column scrolls as before, no horizontal overflow.
- `git diff --check` clean; `node --check` on every changed script.

## Next step

Playtest the tilt as the primary steering skill: if ±10° makes the scripted policy in `experiments/mine-trips.cjs` too strong or too weak, add a tilt-aware policy there and re-measure shift income before considering a wider angle or a tilt cost (heat or wear per adjustment).
