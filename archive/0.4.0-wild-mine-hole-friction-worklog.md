# 0.4.0 Wild Mine — ball frozen beside a hole, rim friction at hole edges

## 1. Review of the ground-memory sleep fix

The previous milestone stopped a spun ball on a pit rim from spinning for
ever: a 0.5 s ground memory keeps rolling resistance alive across the
micro-bounces that skip the supported resolution. Two player reports survived
it:

- "no friction in edges of small holes" — a ball at a rim keeps its coast and
  its spin;
- "ball gets stuck in the geometry of small holes" — a ball stops dead in
  mid-air beside a hole.

The ground memory was not the missing piece. Both symptoms are the same
contact-resolution defect on a rims.

### Root cause A — an "instant" trace swallowed the whole frame

`airStep` branched on `contact.time > 0`. When a trace returned a contact at
(almost) zero time, the step walked to the traced point, called
`resolveContact`, and **returned** - the remaining frame's gravity, travel and
spin were never integrated. The ball repeated that frame for ever: not a
rest, a freeze.

A zero-time contact is unavoidable on a rim, because the field lies:

- the SDF clamps to a one-metre band (`bandMeters` 0.9 m), so a trace whose
  chord dips into the clamp returns `time ≈ 0` with the ball still a hair
  clear of the surface;
- a ball radius is only 1.44 cells and the bilinear field is flat across a
  cell, so a ball touching a corner can read "touching" for ever with no
  closing speed (`v_n ≈ 0`).

Measured: a ball dropped at `(-4.5, 33)` in the start room, next to the right
shelf rim, sat at `y = 33.0572` with `v = (-0.0001, -0.1126)` for the whole
run - the fall velocity frozen exactly as it arrived. The same shape appeared
on dug pits (`x = 0.1866, y = 30.844` for ever) and in rough cave pockets.

### Root cause B — `seated` trusted a stair-stepped scan

`step` re-entered the supported resolution whenever `floorBelow` reported a
floor within `contactSlop`. `floorBelow` walks the **cell occupancy** column,
which is a staircase: under a pocket the ball has fallen into, the occupancy
still reports the shelf above it, so the ball was seated in mid-air and the
roll path then held it there. That is the other half of "stuck".

### Gap C — no friction on a frame with no resolvable contact

While a ball dithered at a rim, no contact was resolvable, so neither the
supported Coulomb/rolling-resistance law nor the rest sleep ever ran. The
coast and any left-over spin survived; that is "no friction at the edges of
small holes". `carryGround` also only carried rolling resistance, never the
slip between spin and travel - a ball spinning at a rim (spin the rim should
absorb into a roll and heat) kept its spin for ever.

## 2. What was implemented (`js/wild-ball.js`)

- **The field is the only surface.** `seated` now tests `sdfAt <= r +
  contactSlop`, not `floorBelow`. The stair-stepped occupancy stays a
  generator/debris query and is no longer collision geometry.
- **A trace is a hint, not a verdict.** `land` moves to the traced point and
  then reads the clearance there: clear by more than `contactSlop` → fly on
  (the corner is charged its rim share); touching → `resolveContact` (bounce
  or roll) with the frame remainder handed to `rollStep`; deeper than `r` →
  separate first, then resolve at the separated centre.
- **A zero-time contact no longer eats the frame.** `airSlicesStep` walks the
  frame in `airSlices` (4) ballistic slices, judging each contact by the
  measured clearance, so a real corner can still be met inside the frame and a
  plateau flies on. Gravity, travel and spin integrate every frame.
- **Two-driver hand-off is bounded.** Going from air to roll used to hand the
  *whole* frame to `rollStep` after the contact had already consumed `time` of
  it, which double-integrated gravity's normal component and popped the ball
  upwards (measured: energy growing), and it recursed
  (`airStep → rollStep → airStep → …`, a stack overflow on release). Hand-offs
  now carry `depth` with `handOffLimit = 2`, and `rollStep` receives only the
  remaining `dt - time`.
- **One friction law, two strengths.** `groundDrag(world, ball, r, dt, share)`
  is the supported law with the step's own weight as the load: rolling
  resistance on the coast, then a Coulomb impulse that drags contact slip
  (`v_t - omega r`) to zero and books the slip work as heat. `carryGround` runs
  it at share 1 across a fresh ground memory; `nibbleGround` runs it at
  `faintNibble = 0.05` on a frame the ball spends in the clear against a rim.
  The faint share stops the "skating at a hole's edge" coast without being felt
  in free flight, and it now drains dithering spin through the slip term.
- **Carried drag stays off the bounce frame.** The frames following a resolved
  contact fly free (`flyFree(..., drag = false)`): that contact's restitution
  and friction already own the step, and a drag impulse there perturbs the
  exact angular-momentum-about-contact invariant (caught by the M3 Coulomb
  test at the 1e-6 level).

## 3. Validation

- `experiments/wild-mine.test.js` — two new regressions, both **failing on the
  previous code** (verified by running the previous `js/wild-ball.js`):
  - `M3 small holes: a ball resting in the clear beside a rim never freezes
    mid-air` - the old code hung at `y = 33.0572`; now the ball falls
    (`y < 32.9`, `|v| > 0.5`).
  - `M3 small holes: a coasting ball carries rim friction into a pit and
    settles in it` - rolls into a 3×2 pit and sleeps inside it.
- `experiments/probe-hole-sweep.cjs` — 24 pit shapes (1×1 … 8×4, rolled in and
  dropped in) plus four drops in the clear: every run reaches rest inside its
  window, no run freezes (`frozen < 30` frames). Full table kept in
  `experiments/logs/0.4.0-wild-mine-hole-sweep.txt`. The five runs still moving
  when their window closes are the passive superball in a pit it nearly fills
  at `e = sqrt(0.9)` (measurements below), not a defect.
- Passive superball in the dug pit: bounce ratios `e = 0.94–0.95` (restitution
  `sqrt(0.9) = 0.9487`), one bounce per ~2.1 s, apex height decaying
  geometrically - the correct, slow `sqrt(0.9)` decay of the design. The M3
  recycling reference settles at 35.5 s with 73 hits and the recycled ball at
  1.6 s with 2 hits, so the "recycling is much faster than passive" assertion
  now holds with a real margin.
- `node --test experiments/*.test.js experiments/*.test.cjs`: **486 / 486
  pass** (34 files).
- Ledger drift stays `≤ 1e-10 J` on every run above.
