# 0.3.9 — Self-consistent perspective and a real debris cap

## Goal

Two shipped settings did not do what their names, their help topics and their
bars promised.

- `PERSPECTIVE` was documented as a view rule: inside the rock band the ball was
  drawn as a *squashed ellipse* (constant width, foreshortened height) while the
  simulation kept a 0.18 m circle. The picture and the physics disagreed, and the
  effect could not be seen at all in normal play — the ball's apex with the full
  autopilot rarely passes 10.9 m, while the warp only starts at 11.375 m.
- `DEBRIS` only affected *future* deposits: a pile is stored in metres, so
  switching 4 → 1 left every existing pile exactly as tall as it was and only
  changed the tile the next grain was drawn with. The ground never followed. The
  0.7 m per-column cap was also a hard "max level" for the piles.

Both are now rules that hold in the picture *and* in the simulation, and the
settings' own help text says which.

## Decisions

- **One law for the chamber.** `SteamCeiling.rockScale(y)` (1× up to 11.375 m,
  a monotone cubic with a mild stretch just inside the warp, 0.25× at the roof)
  is the single description of the perspective. `perspectiveScale(y)` is that
  same number and is what the ball reads.
- **The ball is a body, not a picture.** `physics.ballRadiusAt(y)` is
  `config.radius` times the chamber's scale, so under `Depth scale` the ball's
  collision radius, deck seat, release height, wall and roof clearance, rock
  trace, and the ballistic computer's forecast are all the radius that was
  drawn. `Constant size` is exactly `config.radius` everywhere.
- **The floor game cannot change.** The warp is anchored at the top of the
  chamber, so the scale is exactly 1 below 11.375 m and both settings fly an
  identical ball there; only a ball up in the layer is affected.
- **A step is solved with one body.** `advanceBall` takes its radius at the
  lowest height the frame spans (the smallest body it crosses), and
  `planeContactTime` refines the face contact with the radius at the height the
  contact actually happens, so a contact and the size that made it agree.
  The wall rebound snaps the ball onto the wall it met.
- **Switching DEBRIS re-lays the floor.** `setFloorCompression` scales every
  `floorPiles` entry by `oldSize / newSize` and re-bins nothing: the same cells
  and the same value stand at the new thickness, and the heightmap the ball and
  the platform ride (`floorAt`) moves with the picture. A column the new factor
  would push through the roof keeps what fits and books the rest in `lostCells`
  / `lostValue`, exactly like a deposit at the limit.
- **The roof is the cap.** `pileLimit` is the chamber ceiling (15 m), not the
  0.7 m collector deck: the only level rubble cannot pass is the rock's own
  roof. The old cap is gone from the code, the help topic and the README.
- **A deep pile stays cheap to draw.** A column is charted in at most 64 rows of
  proportionally taller tiles, so a pile at the roof costs no more than a shallow
  one while piles up to 64 tiles keep the exact tile the setting asks for.

## Files

- `js/ceiling.js`: `perspectiveScale`, `setDepthScale`/`state.depthScale`, the
  warp integral shared with `rockDrawnY`, the re-laying
  `setFloorCompression`, `pileLimit = top`, area-budgeted `drawFloor`, and the
  empty-column fix in `updateHeightmap`.
- `js/physics.js`: `ballRadiusAt`, `placementRadius`, `setBallPerspective`, the
  step radius, and the face-contact refinement; every `config.radius` reader in
  a contact now asks for the ball's own radius at that height.
- `js/prediction.js`, `js/autopilot.js`, `js/players.js`: the same ball in the
  forecast, the deck seat and the strike apex.
- `js/main_draw.js`: the ball is a circle at `radius * perspectiveScale(y)`, the
  same number the physics flies; the damage ring keeps its own overlay.
- `js/main_input.js`: both selects call the real setters, reset the autopilot
  plan, and are re-applied on New game (they are chamber rules).
- `js/help.js`, `README.md`: both topics and both bar rows rewritten to the new
  behaviour, and the test summary names the two suites.

## Validation

- `node --test experiments/*.test.js`: 300 / 300 pass.
- New `experiments/depth-scale.test.js` (6 cases): the scale is the cells' own,
  1× below the warp, a quarter at the roof; `Depth scale` resizes the body the
  simulation flies; the same wall and the same deck face are met at the ball's
  own radius at that height, not the classic one; below the warp both settings
  fly an identical ball; the ballistic computer draws the same body.
- `experiments/debris-compression.test.js` rewritten where it encoded the old
  view-only rule: switching re-lays the same material to four times the height
  at 1, the roof is the cap, a setting that would push a column through the roof
  books the excess as lost, and a pile at the roof is charted in 64 rows.
- `experiments/interface.test.js`: the perspective case now asserts a round ball
  at the physics radius, a high ball drawn small, `Constant size` restoring the
  classic body in both layers, and New game keeping the law; the DEBRIS case
  asserts the re-laid pile, the ride height equal to the drawn height, and the
  layer count.
- A 300 s autopilot probe still never lifts the hull above 6 m (max pile 0.56 m
  for 1 205 lost cells), so the removed cap does not bury the machine in normal
  play; a 60 s physics bench is unchanged (479 ms vs 478 ms on the base commit).
- `node --check` on every changed script; `git diff --check` clean.

## Pitfalls found

- `createWorld()` calls `ceiling.reset(1)`, so a test that clears `strength`
  *before* making the world gets the rock back. Clear after the world exists.
- `firstRow` is only refreshed by `updateFirstRow`, and every trace refreshes it,
  so reading it before a call reads a stale row. The heightmap itself now treats
  an empty column as the roof, and `updateHeightmap` multiplies rows by `cell`
  itself so an exhausted column cannot compute a surface below the floor.
- A ball's radius that changes with height makes "the clearance at the start of
  the step" the wrong number for a surface below it. Solve the step with the
  smallest body it spans, and a face with the body at the contact height.
