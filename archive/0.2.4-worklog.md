# 0.2.4 — Actionable tier-II landings and grader call/dismiss

## Implementation

- Changed tier-II prediction to retain the first **deck/ground landing**, not the first contact of any kind. Rock strikes still appear in the extended drawn trajectory and mark its estimate uncertain, but they no longer become impossible platform targets. Later simulated piston bounces remain visible without feeding the autopilot's future movement back into its current target.
- Both intercept switches now use the economical controller whenever Trajectory computer II is available, including uncertain approaches and post-bounce replans. The controller starts toward the landing while the ball is still ascending, then replans immediately after every real bounce. The autopilot target and overlay landing marker therefore describe the same contact, subject only to platform travel limits.
- Replaced the commercial grader's one-way hire action with call/dismiss state. A working grader can be dismissed from anywhere; it stops cutting, drives home with its load, settles the normal 50% share on arrival, and remains parked. Calling during the return cancels dismissal and resumes from the current position without teleporting or losing cargo.
- Added a recessed 1.5 m-wide, 0.25 m-high garage outside the right wall and expanded the chamber camera to contain it. The same grader is drawn at its live position in parked, working and returning states; no inactive-state despawn or wall-clipped parking position remains.
- Updated the equipment button with immediate `Call grader` / `Dismiss grader` labels, `aria-pressed` state, return-cancellation feedback, and change-only readout writes. Updated the research/control descriptions and README.

## Validation

- `node --test experiments/*.test.js` — **111/111 tests pass**.
- `node --check js/main.js`, `js/ceiling.js`, `js/prediction.js`, `js/autopilot.js` and `git diff --check` pass.
- Regression coverage verifies movement during ascent, autopilot/overlay target agreement before and after a bounce, post-bounce economical travel, dismissal without further grading, load settlement at the garage, persistent parked rendering, and the real interface's call/dismiss/cancel-return flow.
- In the seeded two-bounce comparison using identical tier-II landing marks, economical tracking completed both bounces with **610.6 J** reserve draw and **3.90 m/s** peak platform speed; rush-and-brake target following used **2386.4 J** and reached **6.02 m/s**. Neither run grounded the ball, and both preserved the energy ledger.
