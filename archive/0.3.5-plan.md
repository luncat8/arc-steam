# 0.3.5 — Rockets and automatic purchase

## Goal

Give the rig a consumable it buys where it sells, with a standing order that
restocks it, and make its bursts damage the ceiling through small fragments.

## Rules

### Rack and purchase

- Rockets are the rig's own resource, like its road grader. Competitors do not
  carry them, and the controls act only while the rig is the selected player.
- The rack holds 6 rockets. A rocket costs 14 cr.
- Rockets are loaded only while the rig touches a `SELL` wall, at either end of
  the chamber: the same contact that sells the cargo. This is the "shop visit".
- An auto-buy checkbox is a standing order. On each wall visit it buys up to 3
  rockets, after the cargo is sold, as far as the funds and the rack allow.
  A visit lasts while the rig stays on the wall, so a sale that lands during
  the visit can pay for the rest of the batch, and no visit buys a fourth.
- Funds never go negative: every purchase goes through the purse's one debit,
  which refuses a price it cannot cover.
- A manual Buy button loads one rocket at a wall, without a visit cap.
- Auto-buy is the player's order, not world state: New game empties the rack
  and keeps the checkbox. It starts unchecked.
- Communism mode makes rockets free. Test mode loads a full rack as a grant.
- The controls panel shows the stock against the rack on the Fire button, the
  price on the Buy button and the auto-buy state on its checkbox. One status
  line appears only while there is a reason a press does nothing.

### Flight

- `R` or the Fire button launches one rocket; the tube reloads in 0.5 s.
- The launch tube stands in the middle of the collector, so the rock a rocket
  loosens hangs over the bin that fired it.
- A rocket climbs straight up under thrust (3 m/s off the deck, 45 m/s²) and
  reaches the rock in well under a second.
- The fuse is cut for the rock face above the tube and varies uniformly by
  ±20% of that gap. A fuse longer than the gap never fires: the nose reaches
  the rock first and the rocket bursts there. So about half of the rockets
  burst in contact and concentrate their fragments, and half burst early in
  the open and spread them thin.
- With 5% probability the rocket is a dud: its fuse fails, the motor burns out,
  the rocket falls back and bursts on whatever ground it lands on — the floor
  rubble, or the deck of a platform standing there.
- A rocket carries its own chemical energy. Like a competitor's ball it is
  outside the rig's reserve and energy ledger: firing draws nothing from the
  battery and adds no heat, and the energy-balance readout stays closed.

### Burst and fragments

- A burst is a firework: a short flash and 24 shards in an even fan with a
  random phase, 22 m/s ±30%, in one of five colours per burst.
- Shards fly ballistically, lose most of their speed at a side wall, and burn
  out after 1.6 s. A shard that comes down on the ground or a deck is gone.
- A shard that reaches the rock chips it once and drops as a spent ember.
- A chip uses the chamber's one weakening rule, the same as a ball strike:
  strength removed falls off with distance to the power 2.8, a blow takes at
  most half of a cell's remaining strength, and looseness rises with it. The
  shard brings its own kinetic energy (about 6 J, joule for joule with no ball
  multiplier) and its own radius, uniform in 1–4 rock cells.
- Shards release nothing themselves. The weakened rock lets go later through
  the ordinary collapse scheduler and falls as ordinary debris: it is caught,
  lost, piled and sold exactly like rock a ball brought down. A rocket never
  creates or removes material.

### Bounds

- Fixed pools: 8 rockets and 192 shards. A launch is refused unless every
  rocket in the air, the new one included, still has a full fan of free shard
  slots, so a burst is never thinned.
- The step allocates nothing. Fuses, duds and fans come from one seeded stream
  per run, so the same commands replay identically.

## Balance

The target is a rocket that pays for itself in ordinary rock and profits from
everything better than that.

Measured with `experiments/rocket-yield-probe.cjs` (one rocket on a fresh bed,
rig parked under it, 30 s to settle, 21 positions on each of six seeds; log in
`experiments/logs/0.3.5-rocket-yield.txt`):

| per rocket | median | mean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| cells brought down | 3 | 4.2 |
| value brought down, cr | 17.1 | 121 |
| value caught by the parked bin, cr | 14.3 | 92 |
| caught cells at ordinary-rock prices, cr | 11.9 | 14.5 |

"Ordinary rock" is the host stone, the common ores and the filler at their
opening quotes, weighted by abundance. At 14 cr the expected profit in such
rock is about zero (14.5 − 14). The mean of the real catch is far higher
because the weakening rule favours weak minerals and the weak minerals include
the dearest ones: that tail is the intended profit from rich seams. The other
sources of profit are placement — firing under a chosen seam — an engine that
gets the collector back under the falling rock in time, and catching what
falls instead of leaving it to the floor.

Three rockets per wall visit bring down about 13 cells against a 100-cell
cargo, so rockets supplement the ball rather than replace it.

## Implementation

- `js/rockets.js` (`SteamRockets`): rack, purchases, pools, step and drawing.
  `SteamPhysics.step` advances it right after the wall sale;
  `SteamPhysics.createWorld` resets it.
- `js/ceiling.js`: the blow rule is one function, `weaken`, shared by the
  ball's `damage` (owner multiplier and radius) and the shard's `chip` (its own
  energy and radius). `surfaceAt(x)` is the free rock face in the shape a body
  collides with.
- `js/physics.js`: `atSellWall(world)` is the one wall-contact test for the
  sale and the rack.
- Page: a ROCKETS block in the controls panel, the `R` key, a launch tube on
  the rig's collector, the flight pass after the balls, a `rockets` help topic.

## Acceptance

- Auto-buy respects the funds, the visit cap and the rack; the purse never
  goes negative.
- Fuses stay within ±20% of the gap, duds are about 5% and burst on the ground.
- Fragments damage the rock within their radius, the loosened rock falls and is
  booked as ordinary debris, and the rig's energy ledger stays balanced.
- Pools stay bounded under a sustained volley.
