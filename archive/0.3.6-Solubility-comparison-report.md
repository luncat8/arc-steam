### 1. Definitely Worth Picking from GLM

1. **Water Saturation Cap (`sat = max(0, 1 - load / capacity)`) — biggest gameplay win**
   * In our current lab, standing water has no saturation ceiling, so a tiny puddle sitting in a Halite depression could slowly eat indefinitely.
   * GLM multiplies dissolution rate by `(1 - load / (vol * satCap))` (default `60 g/water unit`): **fresh jet flow cuts fast, stagnant brine stalls until pumped away, and downstream salt survives a saturated stream.**
   * That directly rewards driving the platform to pump saturated brine and bringing fresh water to the face, and costs only ~3 lines of math in our Hybrid 2D fluid grid.

2. **Submerged Soluble Debris Still Dissolves (`rate × 3` surface-area multiplier)**
   * GLM caught a real gap in our prototype: once a rock cell breaks into solid debris (e.g. via ball/rockets/sandblaster), crushed Halite / Blue Vitriol / Fizzy grains sitting in water were chemically immortal.
   * Letting resting submerged *soluble* grains dissolve at `~3×` cell rate (higher surface area) creates great **cross-tool synergy**: blast a rich Halite/Vitriol seam with dry tools, then flood the rubble pile so salts dissolve rapidly into pumpable brine while heavy Copper/Iron/Gold stay clean on the floor — while keeping erosion strictly mechanical (`Quartz`/`Sand`/`Gold` never dissolve).

3. **Stoichiometric Fixes for Fizzy Halite & Fluorite (clean chemistry consistency)**
   * **Fizzy Halite (`R₂G₃B`):** GLM pointed out that `R₂G₃B` only contains `1/(2+3+1) = 16.7%` `B`, whereas our v1 table vented `35%`. Easy clean fix: either set `R₂G₃B` vent share to **`16.7%`** (matching the formula), or restate the formula as **`R₂G₂B₂` (`33.3%` `B`)** if we want the louder, riskier wet-mining penalty.
   * **Fluorite (`b 0.62–0.98`) vs. the `B > 0.55` gas-escape law:** GLM proposed an elegant **leach-floor rule**: water leaches only the soluble salt matrix (`~55%` green brine), while the `B`-rich crystal skeleton (`~45%`) never dissolves and survives as solid Fluorite concentrate debris (full value preserved, zero gas lost). That resolves the contradiction cleanly.

4. **Using Existing `strengthInto(mix)` + `debrisLimit (0.8 J)` for Skeleton Collapse**
   * Instead of an arbitrary `solubility >= 0.22` detachment threshold, checking whether the cell's **remaining insoluble skeleton strength** drops below `debrisLimit = 0.8 J` (from `js/rock-materials.js`) ties wet leaching straight into the real game's structural model: trace 15% Halite inside a solid 5 J Quartz wall won't miraculously collapse the Quartz, while 75% Halite cementing 25% Chalcopyrite collapses right on cue and drops the Copper clasts.

5. **Exact Live-Economy Pricing & Market-Consistent Gold Liberation Split**
   * GLM checked our 13-mineral table against `js/rock-materials.js` (`target = 12.5`): `gas` sits at the `150` cap (making wet gas loss brutally clear in credits), `vitriol ≈ 22.0`, `copper ≈ 27.2`, `halite ≈ 2.5`, `fizzy ≈ 3.65`, `gold ≈ 147.35`.
   * **Better Gold-Quartz value split:** If liberated Quartz gangue is priced at plain real `quartz` (`5.21`), then `75%` mass gangue is **`2.65%`** of the lump's value (not `8%`), and the `25%` Pure Gold nugget carries **`97.35%`** (`~573.8/cell`). That gives **exact live-market value conservation without needing a special "gold-gangue quartz" market price**.
   * **Copper family buff catch (`+23%`):** Splitting baseline Copper (`7.83%`) into Chalcopyrite (`4.43%`) + Blue Vitriol (`5.21%` = `9.64%` total) grew Copper because Vitriol's `B` window reached `0.78` (past old Copper's `0.69`). Worth narrowing Vitriol slightly and running `autoAdjust` before live integration.

6. **Per-Mineral Brine Bookkeeping + `rho` / `sol` Fields on `mineralTable`**
   * Storing dissolved load per soluble mineral (`loads[m]`) instead of only two fixed channels (`solHalite`, `solVitriol`) lets the hopper pay each mineral's exact live market quote while computing water tint cleanly from each mineral's `dye` color.
   * Adding `rho` (specific gravity) and `sol` directly onto `js/minerals.js` rows matches `strength` cleanly.

7. **Mode B (`Composition Law f(R,G,B)`) Calibration Tweak**
   * GLM measured that our Mode B `hydrate` smoothstep (`0.18–0.48`) gave `0.68` solubility at Blue Vitriol's center instead of `1.00`. Tightening it slightly to `0.15–0.42` brings Mode A and Mode B into much closer agreement when both are offered as settings.

8. **Nice-to-Have Extras Worth Considering Later**
   * **Salt crust precipitation** when a tiny puddle dries out (conserves mass visually on cave floors if evaporation is enabled).
   * **Lab-only diagnostics:** Beaker immersion mode (Fill/Drain to verify saturation curves cleanly) and a live dissolution-rate strip chart.

### 3. What NOT to Copy from GLM

* **Don't replace the Hybrid 2D fluid solver with GLM's 1D surface-crawling `flows[]` + flat `pool.level`** — it can't handle 2D cave ceilings, ledges, or overhangs, and creates per-frame GC allocations.
* **Don't replace real `js/geology.js` `(R, G, B)` element splits with GLM's synthetic depth-row split** (`Vitriol` upper half / `Chalcopyrite` lower half in `genFieldScene`) — element-driven generation is the whole foundation of `arc-steam`'s geology (if we ever want oxidized shallow vitriol later, that belongs in `0.4.0` depth bias on `B`, not hardcoded rows).
* **Don't lose probabilistic whole-cell ceiling detachment** — GLM's continuous fractional mix decay per cell works neatly in a tiny `120 × 34` benchtop grid, whereas in the full game (shooting upward at ceilings), probabilistic surface-cell completion with `debrisLimit`-checked skeleton collapse is faster, cleaner for `SteamCeiling`, and feels much more tactile.
