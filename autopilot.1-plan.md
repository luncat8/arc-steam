# autopilot.1 — Four assisted duties over the manual controls

Source: the `draft.txt` autopilot note (checkboxes and shortcuts), the 0.1.6 rule that autonomous interception must weigh feasibility, energy, heat and wear rather than chase at maximum acceleration, and the measured cost of manual chasing in `experiments/logs/0.1.8-mine-trips.txt`.

## Decisions

- The autopilot is four independent duties, each a checkbox in the controls panel and a keyboard shortcut: chase the predicted landing (`X`), timed intercept with economy braking (`shift`+`X`), drive keeper (`Z`), level bounce (`C`). Checkboxes because the draft asked for them and because they show state at a glance; shortcuts because interception is a mid-flight decision and the hand is on the keyboard.
- No research gate and no purchase. The duties are operating policy on machinery the player already owns, and they are the control that makes the energy trade-offs legible; a lock would hide exactly the comparison the design wants the player to see. Whether a later technology node should gate them is an open decision, not this one.
- Every duty yields to the manual command that does the same job: `A`/`D` or a travel click takes the intercept back, `Space`/`Q` or the drive button takes the pulse back, and the slider, wheel or open-air drag takes the tilt back. The enabled checkbox stays checked. The matching duty is suspended while movement or tilt is held and for two seconds of idle simulation time after release or the last discrete input. The status clause says `manual override`, then returns to its plan. Only an explicit checkbox or autopilot shortcut disables the duty. Pausing freezes the grace period; reset clears pending overrides and held input.
- One intercept duty at a time. Chase and timed intercept both own the platform's travel command, so engaging one clears the other instead of letting two policies fight over `world.target` and `world.direction`.
- The chase aims the **reflector centre** (`predicted landing − pistonCentre`) on the landing, not the platform centre: only the right-hand face bounces, so centring the platform parks the landing 0.63 m left of the face centre and leaves only 0.54 m of face before the collector pocket takes over.
- The timed intercept spends the whole predicted flight time getting to the mark instead of rushing and braking. It does not need to stop there: being at the mark when the ball arrives is enough, and rolling through it is the cheaper run when the distance is short. The commanded speed is the stop-run peak `(a·T − sqrt(a²T² − 4·a·reach)) / 2` for `reach = |distance| − arrival tolerance` whenever that discriminant exists, otherwise the slower of a constant roll and the brake-limited approach speed. Rolling is allowed only below 0.5 m/s, so a long intercept still stops and holds the mark while the ball is elsewhere.
- Recuperation shapes the braking style. With the brake flywheel installed, braking returns energy to the reserve, so the timed duty holds a tight 0.08 m/s speed deadband and brakes promptly; without it the same brake dumps the kinetic energy as heat, so the deadband widens to 0.3 m/s and the duty coasts longer. Slow recuperative braking is preferred over a fast stop that loses the energy.
- The drive keeper prices the *next* piston contact with the same solver the simulation uses: it compares the passive rebound apex (at the current tilt, or at the tilt the level bounce is heading to) against `ceiling − radius`, and switches the pulse on only when the passive bounce would fall more than a quarter of a pulse of apex short, and off above the ceiling line. That holds the ball within one pulse of the top instead of letting it run away, and keeps the ball slow enough to intercept. The keeper also releases a caught ball, so storage never becomes a parking lot.
- The level bounce bisects the ±10° range for the tilt that leaves the next piston contact travelling straight up, then walks toward that tilt at the actuator's own slew limit (45°/s) through the single angle writer, so the face never snaps, never exceeds its range, and never out-runs the piston the player can see. When no piston contact is planned it holds the wanted tilt instead of hunting on stale information.
- Prediction uncertainty is handled explicitly: a horizon that ends in rock or at the height-and-gravity-derived prediction horizon has no landing to meet, so the intercept duties track the ball's own horizontal position (minus the reflector offset) rather than coasting the platform away on a missing target, and the panel reports the intercept as in progress instead of promising a mark.
- The piston contact solve is one exported function (`physics.solvePiston`) shared by the simulation step, the drive keeper's pricing and the tilt bisection. Three consumers of one solver is what keeps the autopilot's decisions consistent with what the ball will actually do, and it is the reason the exported solver is now covered by a test that steps the real simulation.

## Design

- `js/autopilot.js` owns policy only: no DOM, no rendering, no allocation per step (one reused solver scratch). It reads the world and `SteamPrediction`, and writes through the simulation's own writers: `world.target`/`world.direction` for travel, `world.drive` plus `physics.launch` for the pulse, `physics.setPistonAngle` for tilt. It replans at 20 Hz or when the ball's state, bounce count or push count changes; between plans it only re-evaluates the speed deadband and walks the slew-limited tilt.
- `SteamAutopilot.state` is a frozen live view (`move`, `smart`, `drive`, `angle`, `active`, `interceptX`, `contactIn`, `wantedDrive`, `wantedAngle`) for the panel and the tests; `set`, `toggle`, `override`, `hold`, `suspended`, `reset`, `update` and `channels` are the commands. Move and smart share a single override timer; drive and angle each have an independent timer. Expiry forces a fresh plan before automatic commands resume.
- `js/main.js` adds the four checkboxes, the one-clause-per-duty status line (`#autopilot-status`, hidden when nothing is engaged) and the shortcuts. Checkbox change, key press and manual override all pass through the same `setChannel` / `toggleChannel` / `override` / `hold` writers, so the checkboxes always show the truth and the write-only-on-change discipline of the panel is preserved.
- `js/physics.js` exports `solvePiston(world, vx, vy, angle, push, out)` plus the `contactResult()` scratch it fills, and adds `pistonSlewRate` (π/4 rad/s) to the config as the actuator limit the level bounce obeys. `step` resolves piston contacts through the same exported call, so simulation and autopilot cannot disagree about a contact.

## Acceptance

- The four checkboxes engage and disengage their duty and always show the live state; `X`, `shift`+`X`, `Z` and `C` toggle the same duties; engaging one intercept duty clears the other.
- A manual command suspends only the matching duty without changing enabled preferences. Holding input outlasts the grace period; releasing or ending discrete input resumes control after two idle simulation seconds. Repeated input renews only its own timer. Explicit switch-off during a suspension never re-enables itself.
- Both intercept duties put the reflector face under the predicted landing before contact, and the timed duty's peak platform speed and reserve draw are far below the chase's for the same intercept.
- With intercept + keeper + level bounce over many bounces the ball's apex stays between the rock underside and the ceiling line plus one pulse of apex, the drive is observed both armed and held, and an always-on drive exceeds that band.
- The level bounce leaves post-bounce horizontal speed near zero, never slews faster than 45°/s and never leaves the ±10° range.
- The energy ledger stays exact: the balance error remains at integration noise (~1e-9 J) in every autopilot run.

## Historical calibration (10 m chamber, 1 / 1.5 ball speed scale)

`node experiments/autopilot-trips.cjs` runs the same seeded 120 s shift under each policy; output lives in `experiments/logs/autopilot.1-trips.txt`.

- The timed intercept halves the cost of a full shift: `X z c` draws 6182 J against 13057 J for `x z c` over the same 78–79 m of travel, because rushing and braking costs peak speed (kinetic energy dumped as heat), not distance.
- With the brake flywheel installed the same timed shift draws 5277 J (150.8 J per bounce) against 10230 J for the chase: recuperation is worth about half the draw, which is why the timed duty tightens its deadband when recovery exists.
- The drive keeper holds the apex at 13.1–13.7 m against the 9.82 m ceiling line (one 36 J pulse above it) and the average ball speed near 10.4 m/s, where manual play runs the ball to a 24.4 m apex and 14.3 m/s.
- The level bounce leaves mean post-bounce horizontal speed at 0.00–0.14 m/s while slewing at its 45°/s limit; on this seed the residual drift always leaves the vertical root at the range end, so the tilt parks at ±10° here and settles mid-range on others.
- Groundings (2–7 per shift) come from sloped rock rebounds that throw the ball about 3 m in under a second, which no intercept can reach at the 0.6 force setting. The prediction status reports the shortfall in metres instead of promising the catch, and the gentler keeper regime mines less per shift in exchange for a slower, cheaper ball.
