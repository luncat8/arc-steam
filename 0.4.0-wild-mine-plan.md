# 0.4.0 Wild Mine — plan

A second game mode beside the classic chamber. The chamber is a fixed 24 × 15 m box
over a floor heightmap; Wild Mine is an open, seeded rock section where the cave
itself is the floor, the ceiling and every wall.

Players: **P1 = platform** (the existing machine) and **P2 = ball** (a new player
kind, not a platform). No co-op/versus split, no win condition: both are commands
over one shared world, and players use them as they like.

Classic mode must keep playing exactly as it does now. Every rule below lives
behind a mode guard.

## 1. Non-goals

- No win/lose state, no match clock, no teams.
- No rockets, sandblaster, water gun or grader in the first playable slice. They
  are re-attached after the cave world and both players are stable (M6).
- No new material table, no second economy: `js/minerals.js`,
  `js/rock-materials.js`, `js/geology*.js` and the chamber market stay the one
  source of strength, colour and price.

## 2. Decisions (resolves the open questions in the draft)

**D1 — Ball energy source: onboard battery, charged by the platform and bounce recuperation.**
The ball carries its own battery (`ballBattery`, joules) and its own engine.
Spin-motor torque and the jump actuator draw from it. Primary recharging is by
induction while the ball is held or resting on the platform's deck: the
platform's reserve pays through `physics.spend`, waste becomes heat. An optional
bounce-recycling toggle acts like the platform's regenerative braking flywheel,
actively damping rock and deck rebounds to stop bouncing quickly and returning
`55%` of the damped normal energy to `ballBattery` at `90%` efficiency (capped
by battery capacity, remainder as heat). This keeps P1 and P2 coupled, keeps one
energy ledger, and needs no free energy source. A flat ball can still be scooped
or nudged by the platform, so it is never stranded.

**D2 — Bounce loss is 10% of normal impact energy.** Restitution
`e = sqrt(0.9)`. Rock contact and deck contact share the rule; the retained
tangential component carries the roll. The 10% that is lost is split between heat
and rock damage (damage first, capped by the strength actually removed, the
remainder as heat) — the same split the chamber already books in
`passiveImpact`/`rockPassiveImpact`.

**D3 — Damage is contact-force driven, energy booked.** For a contact of the
ball against the surface normal `n` over one step, the impulse is
`J = m (1 + e) v_n`; the useful fracture energy is
`E = ½ m v_n² (1 − e²)` and is what `cave.weaken` receives. Momentum, not energy,
decides *whether* a contact is a hit (a graze with no normal impulse does no
damage), which is what "compute damage from the contact force" means in practice.

**D4 — Spin motor, rolling inertia and Coulomb bounce friction.** The ball is a
solid sphere with `I = 2/5 m r²` and clockwise angular velocity `ω`. The motor
applies torque directly to `ω` both on a surface and in mid-air: without surface
friction or translational mass (`I` alone vs rolling inertia `I + m r² = 7/5 m r²`),
`ω` spins up `3.5×` faster in mid-air and tapers by back-EMF up to the unloaded
`motorFreeSpeed`. Every surface contact (roll or bounce, on floor, wall, ceiling
or deck) applies a Coulomb friction impulse along the oriented tangent
`t = (n_y, −n_x)` opposing contact slip `v_t − ω r`, clamped to `±μ J_n` with
effective tangential mass `2/7 m`. Angular momentum about the contact point is
preserved; fast spin skids on light impacts and carries leftover spin into the
next bounce, a pure roll (`v_t = ω r`) loses nothing to static friction, and
kinetic slip work is booked as heat. A small `rollingResistance` settles a
coasting roll into heat. A wall or overhang must not steal that floor roll: if
the ball is still supported from below, Coulomb friction and rolling resistance
keep acting on the floor tangent so leftover spin dies instead of freewheeling
while the body sits. Rough ground breaks a roll into micro-bounces that skip
the supported resolution almost every frame, so rolling resistance would never
integrate: keep a `groundMemoryTime` (0.5 s) window of the last rock contact
(its tangent and the strongest upward share of any normal seen in the window —
a sideways touch must not poison it) and keep applying rolling resistance
between contacts while the window is fresh, never on the bounce frame itself,
which belongs to the restitution/friction resolution. Once both centre speed
and rim speed fall under `restSpeed`
with the motor off, the ball sleeps (ω and v snap to 0, leftover kinetic booked
as heat). Jump pulses act strictly along the contact normal and preserve both
`v_t` and `ω`.

**D5 — Surface classification.** The signed field is the bare-rock collision
surface; a soil cover supplies its own crest (D9). Occupancy (`floorBelow`) is a stair-stepped scan and reports a shelf
floor under a pocket the ball has fallen into, so it may not seat a ball; the
SDF clearance decides. A trace is a hint, never a verdict: it is a root of
`sdf - r` sampled at half-cell steps and the field clamps to a one-metre band,
so a trace can land on the band edge (or on a plateau, since a ball radius is
only 1.44 cells) with the ball still clear. The step therefore moves to the
traced point and reads the clearance there — clear by more than `contactSlop`
flies on, touching bounces or rolls, deeper than `r` is separated first and
then resolved. A contact at almost zero time (the ring artefact, a real
overlap or a corner a hair ahead) is never resolved blind: the frame is walked
in `airSlices` (4) ballistic slices, so gravity, travel and spin integrate
every frame and a real corner can still be met inside it. A contact is a *roll*
while the surface
curvature over the contact patch keeps the centre trajectory continuous: the
normal changes by less than `curvatureLimit` (30°) over one ball diameter, and the
ball's `v_n` at the contact is below `bounceFloor` (0.35 m/s). Otherwise the
contact is a *hit*: restitution, damage and the 10% loss apply. A convex corner
tighter than the ball radius is always a hit because the ball cannot wrap it —
the SDF normal turns faster than the ball can accelerate, which is exactly the
"sharp curvature change acts as a wall" rule. A contact that is already touching
at the start of a step is resolved as an ordinary hit or roll at the separated
centre, never as a silent push: an overlap may not swallow a fast inward normal,
only the push itself is booked (as imported work). Because the floor applies to
the incoming normal speed, a ball dropped straight down keeps bouncing until that
speed falls under `bounceFloor` — about a dozen bounces at `e = sqrt(0.9)`, which
is the price of the chamber's 10% loss being a *superball* rather than felt.
A frame that resolves a contact may hand the rest of the frame to the other
driver (a landing rolls, a roll that leaves the ground flies); the hand-off
carries only the remaining time — never the whole `dt`, which would integrate
gravity twice — and is capped at `handOffLimit` (2) so the two drivers cannot
bounce one frame between them. Friction has one law with two strengths,
`groundDrag(share)`: rolling resistance on the coast plus a Coulomb impulse
that drags contact slip (`v_t − ω r`) to zero. Share 1 is carried across the
ground memory; `faintNibble` (0.05) is charged on a frame the ball spends in
the clear at a rim, which is where the coast and any dithering spin die
instead of skating along a hole's edge. Carried drag never runs on a frame
right after a resolved contact: that contact's restitution and friction own
the step, and an extra tangential impulse there breaks the exact
angular-momentum-about-contact invariant.

**D6 — Cave representation: 2D occupancy + derived SDF, chunked in X.**
One `Uint8Array` occupancy grid per chunk (1 = solid). A signed distance field in
metres is derived from it (exact squared-Euclidean transform, clamped to a 1 m
band) and refreshed locally after every mined cell. Use the SDF for bare-rock contact. Query the same occupancy for
liquid boundaries and for the faces supporting the soil heightmaps; loose soil is
not written back as solid rock. Occupancy, strength and damage stay separate,
so generated void, mined void (`dug` bit) and intact rock are distinguishable
(`gas-caves.1-plan.md`).

**D7 — Vertical scale is a log-style law anchored on P2.** Each world height is
drawn at the scale `s(y)`, the drawing map is its integral, and the far edge of
the view is `0.25×`. A hyperbolic reciprocal law
`s(u) = 1 / (1 + 3u)`, `u = |y − y_ball| / span`, is used instead of a literal
`log`: it has the same "far field flattens" shape, but its integral
`ln(1 + 3u)/3` is closed-form, so the draw map and its inverse both exist in a few
lines, exactly as the chamber's cubic warp does. Scale is 1 at the ball and never
below 0.25 within the view.

**D8 — Camera follows the average of both players, smoothly.** Target =
`(x_platform + x_ball)/2, (y_platform + y_ball)/2` (a lone player's own pose), and
the camera eases towards it with a critically damped follow at `cameraRate`
(2.5 /s). The vertical scale anchor is the active ball's `y` (D7), so the ball
always reads at 1×.

**D9 — Debris is a soil heightmap on each supporting face.** Only grains in
flight are particles. A bin is keyed by absolute column and supporting rock row:
several galleries can occupy the same x without sharing any material. At rest
its height is `cells × cell / compression`. Use the chamber's 1–3–3–1 vertex
stencil on connected shelves, including unfilled shoulder columns. A buried
one-cell stair can join a run; a shaft, overhang or uncovered step ends it.
Clip to local rock headroom. The renderer, ball contact, platform ground and
grains in flight use the same piecewise-linear crest. If a support opens, move
its cells and value down to the next face, merging with any soil already there.
Only what cannot fit is lost. The blade transports and shaves cells with their
proportional value, not just their visible thickness.

Soil is indestructible. A hit on the crest passes at most 20% of the usual
fracture budget into the backing rock, unfocused over 4× the ordinary radius
at and below the face. Book the unspent bounce loss as heat. DEBRIS uses
`setFloorCompression` (1..8, half-step resolution); Wild Mine defaults to 1.5,
Classic to 4, with separate choices retained on mode changes and New game.

**D10 — One cart, called automatically.** Wild Mine has no selling walls, so a
service cart runs on the cave floor: `idle → dispatched → docked → loading →
returning → selling → idle`. It is dispatched when cargo is full or on demand; it
buys the load at the live market quote and books one `sell` ledger entry, then
drives back. It reuses `ceiling.sellCargo` and `ceiling.ledger`; it is the same
pattern as the garage grader in `js/ceiling.js`, and its route is a sequence of
`floorBelow` waypoints, not a scripted line.

## 3. World model (`js/cave.js`)

Units: metres, y up, row 0 at the bottom.

| constant | value | why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `cell` | 0.125 m | same rock cell as the chamber |
| `rows` | 512 | 64 m vertical section |
| `chunkColumns` | 128 | 16 m chunk, streaming granularity |
| `bandCells` | 8 | 1 m SDF band, wider than the largest body radius |
| `curvatureLimit` | 30° | roll vs hit (D5) |

Per chunk (all typed, all reused on refill):

```
solid   Uint8Array   1 = standing rock
dug     Uint8Array   1 = a solid cell removed by mining (never by generation)
damage  Float32Array accumulated looseness 0..1, including sub-byte diffuse hits
sampled Uint8Array   strength has been read for this cell (a cache, not state)
strength Float32Array remaining strength in J per cell, 0 = open
sdf     Float32Array signed distance in metres, negative inside rock
```

Mineral mix, element colours and price are **not** stored: they are a pure
function of absolute geological coordinates, recomputed on demand by
`mixAt(column, row, out)` / `strengthAt`. This keeps a chunk at ~1.1 MB and one
market (`js/ceiling.js`) the only pricer.

Strength is read lazily on the first blow at a cell: the shared geology sampler
costs about 2.5 µs per cell, which is minutes of CPU across a fresh chunk, while
generation needs only occupancy. `sampled` records what has been read, so an
evicted unedited chunk is still regenerated identically and an edited one keeps
exactly the cells it had touched.

Generation is a pure function of `(seed, absoluteColumn, row)`: any chunk can be
rebuilt identically, so streaming is cache management, never state.

- Two value-noise octaves from `geology.sampleNoise`, flattened through
  `geology.flatten`: `chamber` (freq 0.02 and 0.054) and `vein` (freq 0.035, ridged
  `1 − |2n − 1|`).
- Void where `chamber > chamberThreshold` (0.72) or `vein > veinThreshold`
  (0.90). Rock stays solid within 2 rows of `y = 0` and `y = 64`.
- Start room carved around `spawn = (0, 32)`: an ellipse of 6.4 × 3.8 m (25% of
  the classic 25.8 × 15 m view), its edge eroded by noise; inside it a flat shelf
  from `spawnY − 1.1` down, 8 m wide, with 2.4 m of headroom. The platform needs
  `≥ 3.6 m` of span and this guarantees 8 m on the first frame.
- Strength of every solid cell: `materials.strengthInto(mix)` from
  `geology.fillElements`/`fillMix`, the same two calls `ceiling.populateCell`
  makes — identical rock, one table.

SDF: two exact squared-Euclidean distance transforms (row pass + column pass),
one for the solid mask and one for its complement, over a chunk padded by the
band on both sides so the transform sees the neighbour's rock. Distances are
between cell centres, so half a cell is taken off: the zero level then lies on
the cell boundary a body collides with, instead of half a cell inside the rock.
`sdf = ±max(0, d·cell − cell/2)`, clamped to `±bandMeters`, negative inside.
Normals are the normalised central-difference gradient of `sdf`, computed on
demand (no per-normal storage).

Edits: `weaken` and `dig` set cells open, set `dug`, and refresh the SDF in the
`bandCells`-wide rectangle around the change by ring search — the same values the
chunk-wide transform produces, without rebuilding the chunk.

Streaming: a map of live chunks plus an `edited` set. A chunk outside the camera
window is dropped unless it was edited; an edited chunk is kept in a small store
and restored on return. Generation is capped at `maxChunksPerFrame` (1) after the
initial window, so a fast camera opens rock a frame ahead of the platform instead
of stalling.

Queries (no allocation, caller-owned scratch):

```
solidAt(x, y)               -> boolean
sdfAt(x, y)                 -> metres, bilinear over cell centres
normalAt(x, y, out)         -> unit outward normal
traceCircle(x, y, vx, vy, t, r, g, out) -> earliest contact {time,x,y,nx,ny}
floorBelow(x, y)            -> world y of the first solid top surface below
roofAbove(x, y)             -> world y of the first solid bottom surface above
spanAt(x, y)                -> void width in metres through that point
weaken(x, y, E, gain, radius, diffuse) -> spent fracture joules; opened faces reported as a batch
```

## 4. Game rules

Platform (P1): the existing machine, driven by its existing controller and
autopilot. Differences: piston range `±90°` instead of `±10°`, no side walls and
no chamber roof and no charge pad. The ball recharges by deck induction; the
platform burns collected fuel (see `0.4.0-wild-mine-tools-plan.md`). Ground comes
from the local `floorBelow` face plus its soil crest at each wheel; a platform needs a `≥ 3.6 m` span, so it can never
rest on a knife edge (it slides off sloped ground by the existing pose solve).

Ball (P2): `{ kind: 'ball', id: 'ball', x, y, vx, vy, omega, battery, jump, recycle }`.
Commands:
- `left` / `right` motor (`←` / `→` or held panel buttons): spin torque from the
  battery (opposing spin brakes into heat). On rock, Coulomb friction converts
  spin into rolling; in mid-air the ball remains a ballistic projectile in `(x, y)`
  while `ω` accelerates freely `3.5×` faster up to `motorFreeSpeed / r`, then
  transfers into tangential velocity via Coulomb friction on the next bounce.
- `jump` actuator (`↑` or the `Jump actuator on/off` button, styled and driven
  like P1's `Drive on/off` piston button): while enabled, feeds a normal energy
  pulse (`½ m jumpSpeed²`) from the battery into every rock contact — lifting off
  a rolling surface and boosting each rebound (`v_n' = sqrt(v_n² + 2E/m)`).
- `recycle` checkbox (`↓` or `Recycle bounce energy` switch): actively damps
  rebounds (`recycleDamping = 0.88`, settling into a roll once under `bounceFloor`)
  and returns `55% × 90%` of the damped normal energy into the ball's battery,
  keeping tangential roll intact. Engaging `jump` clears `recycle` and vice versa.
`A` / `D` remain P1's drive.

Release: the ball leaves the collector face along the piston normal with
`launchSpeed` paid by its own battery (a flat battery still lets it drop), so the
`±90°` tilt aims the launch; `jump` and `recycle` keep the player's setting
across release. A held ball or one resting on the deck charges at `chargePower`;
the platform's reserve pays through `physics.spend`, the induction loss becomes
heat. A deck contact after cooldown catches the ball back into the collector, so
a run always ends with a recharge. The deck bounce shares D2's restitution,
Coulomb spin friction and optional bounce recuperation.

Battery & contacts: `batteryCapacity = 300 J`, `chargePower = 60 W` at 85%
induction, `surfaceFriction = 0.85`, motor `2.5 m/s²` (rolling equivalent;
`8.75 m/s²` rim acceleration in air) full torque to `5 m/s` tapering to `8 m/s`
free rim speed at 80%, jump actuator pulse `2.6 m/s` (`10.95 J` on stock mass)
at 80%, launch `3.2 m/s` at 80%, bounce recycling `88%` rebound damping with
`55%` recovery at `90%` efficiency, `rollingResistance = 0.35 m/s²` and
`restSpeed = 0.04 m/s` so a motor-off ball on land sleeps instead of spinning
in place. Every draw, recovery and waste share is booked through the one ledger
(`world.heat`, `world.fractureEnergy`, `world.importedEnergy`).

Damage: pass the fracture budget in joules before gain. A direct blow focuses
up to half its strength budget on the contact cell, and distributes the rest
over rock within the falloff radius. No cell loses more than half its remaining
strength to one blow. A cell below `debrisLimit` (0.8 J) with at least 0.3
looseness releases if it has a free cardinal face; snapshot the free faces
before opening any. Buried loose cells stay weak until exposed and struck;
section border rows stay solid. Only openings require an SDF refresh.

Debris: for each fractured cell emit its mineral mass and break-time value
once, whether a ball or a shard did the mining. The collector can intercept a
grain before it hits the next ledge; otherwise settle it into that ledge's bin
(D9). Cave rock potential is outside the ball's ledger; the work spent
weakening rock and the mechanical effect of catching cargo are booked.

## 6. Invariants to test

1. Generation determinism: same seed, same chunk, byte-identical; a column on a
   chunk seam equals the neighbour's copy of it.
2. `sdf` sign and magnitude: solid `≤ 0`, void `≥ 0`, distance within `1e-6` of a
   brute-force reference inside the band; patch refresh equals a full rebuild.
3. `traceCircle` never returns a contact inside rock, and a dropped ball lands at
   `surface + radius`.
4. `weaken` never removes strictly interior rock or section border rows, and
   never removes more strength than the energy brings.
5. Energy: ball kinetic + rotational + potential + battery + heat + fracture is
   constant across a bouncy shot (the chamber's `totalEnergy` pattern).
6. Streaming: an edited chunk evicted and re-entered holds exactly its edits.
7. Debris rest: the filled polygon, wheels and swept impacts agree on the crest,
   even on its tapered shoulders. Separate stacked galleries, re-seat a mined
   support, and conserve cells/value through grading and compression (including
   any explicitly booked cap losses).
