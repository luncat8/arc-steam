# 0.4.0 Wild Mine — plan

A second game mode beside the classic chamber. The chamber is a fixed 24 × 15 m box
over a floor heightmap; Wild Mine is an open, seeded rock section where the cave
itself is the floor, the ceiling and every wall.

Players: **P1 = platform** (the existing machine) and **P2 = ball** (a new player
kind, not a platform). No co-op/versus split, no win condition: both are commands
over one shared world, and players use them as they like.

Classic mode must keep playing exactly as it does now. Every rule below lives
behind a mode guard.

## 1. Non-goals

- No win/lose state, no match clock, no teams.
- No rockets, sandblaster, water gun or grader in the first playable slice. They
  are re-attached after the cave world and both players are stable (M6).
- No new material table, no second economy: `js/minerals.js`,
  `js/rock-materials.js`, `js/geology*.js` and the chamber market stay the one
  source of strength, colour and price.

## 2. Decisions (resolves the open questions in the draft)

**D1 — Ball energy source: onboard battery, charged by the platform.**
The ball carries its own battery (`ballBattery`, joules) and its own engine.
Motor torque and jump impulse draw from it. It recharges by induction only while
it touches or rolls on the platform's deck: the platform's reserve pays, the ball
receives it through the same `physics.spend`-style booking, waste becomes heat.
This keeps P1 and P2 coupled (the ball must come home between runs), keeps one
energy ledger, and needs no free "atomic" source. A flat ball can still be
scooped or nudged by the platform, so it is never stranded.

**D2 — Bounce loss is 10% of normal impact energy.** Restitution
`e = sqrt(0.9)`. Rock contact and deck contact share the rule; the retained
tangential component carries the roll. The 10% that is lost is split between heat
and rock damage (damage first, capped by the strength actually removed, the
remainder as heat) — the same split the chamber already books in
`passiveImpact`/`rockPassiveImpact`.

**D3 — Damage is contact-force driven, energy booked.** For a contact of the
ball against the surface normal `n` over one step, the impulse is
`J = m (1 + e) v_n`; the useful fracture energy is
`E = ½ m v_n² (1 − e²)` and is what `cave.weaken` receives. Momentum, not energy,
decides *whether* a contact is a hit (a graze with no normal impulse does no
damage), which is what "compute damage from the contact force" means in practice.

**D4 — Rolling preserves linear impulse and rotational moment.** The ball has
`I = 2/5 m r²` and an angular velocity `ω`. A no-slip contact applies a friction
impulse that exchanges `v` and `ω` at fixed total kinetic energy; `ω` is used for
the drawn spin and for the "sharp curvature" test. Jump (an impulse along the
contact normal) preserves both `v_t` and `ω`.

**D5 — Surface classification.** A contact is a *roll* while the surface
curvature over the contact patch keeps the centre trajectory continuous: the
normal changes by less than `curvatureLimit` (30°) over one ball diameter, and the
ball's `v_n` at the contact is below `bounceFloor` (0.35 m/s). Otherwise the
contact is a *hit*: restitution, damage and the 10% loss apply. A convex corner
tighter than the ball radius is always a hit because the ball cannot wrap it —
the SDF normal turns faster than the ball can accelerate, which is exactly the
"sharp curvature change acts as a wall" rule.

**D6 — Cave representation: 2D occupancy + derived SDF, chunked in X.**
One `Uint8Array` occupancy grid per chunk (1 = solid). A signed distance field in
metres is derived from it (exact squared-Euclidean transform, clamped to a 1 m
band) and refreshed locally after every mined cell. The SDF is the single surface
query for collision, debris resting, water depth and platform ground: there is no
second terrain model. Occupancy, strength and damage stay three separate arrays,
so generated void, mined void (`dug` bit) and intact rock are distinguishable
(`gas-caves.1-plan.md`).

**D7 — Vertical scale is a log-style law anchored on P2.** Each world height is
drawn at the scale `s(y)`, the drawing map is its integral, and the far edge of
the view is `0.25×`. A hyperbolic reciprocal law
`s(u) = 1 / (1 + 3u)`, `u = |y − y_ball| / span`, is used instead of a literal
`log`: it has the same "far field flattens" shape, but its integral
`ln(1 + 3u)/3` is closed-form, so the draw map and its inverse both exist in a few
lines, exactly as the chamber's cubic warp does. Scale is 1 at the ball and never
below 0.25 within the view.

**D8 — Camera follows the average of both players, smoothly.** Target =
`(x_platform + x_ball)/2, (y_platform + y_ball)/2` (a lone player's own pose), and
the camera eases towards it with a critically damped follow at `cameraRate`
(2.5 /s). The vertical scale anchor is the active ball's `y` (D7), so the ball
always reads at 1×.

**D9 — Debris is indestructible and damped.** A debris cell that is hit absorbs
the hit: its own velocity changes elastically-enough to stay put, and the solid
below it receives `debrisPassThrough` (20%) of the fracture energy spread over
`4×` the normal damage radius. Debris rests on the SDF surface it lands on, any
orientation, and rolls off steep ones. Y compression is the existing
`setFloorCompression` setting, exposed in every mode as `1..8` in half steps with
`1.5` as the Wild Mine default.

**D10 — One cart, called automatically.** Wild Mine has no selling walls, so a
service cart runs on the cave floor: `idle → dispatched → docked → loading →
returning → selling → idle`. It is dispatched when cargo is full or on demand; it
buys the load at the live market quote and books one `sell` ledger entry, then
drives back. It reuses `ceiling.sellCargo` and `ceiling.ledger`; it is the same
pattern as the garage grader in `js/ceiling.js`, and its route is a sequence of
`floorBelow` waypoints, not a scripted line.

## 3. World model (`js/cave.js`)

Units: metres, y up, row 0 at the bottom.

| constant | value | why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `cell` | 0.125 m | same rock cell as the chamber |
| `rows` | 512 | 64 m vertical section |
| `chunkColumns` | 128 | 16 m chunk, streaming granularity |
| `bandCells` | 8 | 1 m SDF band, wider than the largest body radius |
| `curvatureLimit` | 30° | roll vs hit (D5) |

Per chunk (all typed, all reused on refill):

```
solid   Uint8Array   1 = rock or debris-bearing solid
dug     Uint8Array   1 = a solid cell removed by mining (never by generation)
damage  Uint8Array   looseness 0..255, as the chamber's damageField
sampled Uint8Array   strength has been read for this cell (a cache, not state)
strength Float32Array remaining strength in J per cell, 0 = open
sdf     Float32Array signed distance in metres, negative inside rock
```

Mineral mix, element colours and price are **not** stored: they are a pure
function of absolute geological coordinates, recomputed on demand by
`mixAt(column, row, out)` / `strengthAt`. This keeps a chunk at ~1.5 MB and one
market (`js/ceiling.js`) the only pricer.

Strength is read lazily on the first blow at a cell: the shared geology sampler
costs about 2.5 µs per cell, which is minutes of CPU across a fresh chunk, while
generation needs only occupancy. `sampled` records what has been read, so an
evicted unedited chunk is still regenerated identically and an edited one keeps
exactly the cells it had touched.

Generation is a pure function of `(seed, absoluteColumn, row)`: any chunk can be
rebuilt identically, so streaming is cache management, never state.

- Two value-noise octaves from `geology.sampleNoise`, flattened through
  `geology.flatten`: `chamber` (freq 0.02 and 0.054) and `vein` (freq 0.035, ridged
  `1 − |2n − 1|`).
- Void where `chamber > chamberThreshold` (0.72) or `vein > veinThreshold`
  (0.90). Rock stays solid within 2 rows of `y = 0` and `y = 64`.
- Start room carved around `spawn = (0, 32)`: an ellipse of 6.4 × 3.8 m (25% of
  the classic 25.8 × 15 m view), its edge eroded by noise; inside it a flat shelf
  from `spawnY − 1.1` down, 8 m wide, with 2.4 m of headroom. The platform needs
  `≥ 3.6 m` of span and this guarantees 8 m on the first frame.
- Strength of every solid cell: `materials.strengthInto(mix)` from
  `geology.fillElements`/`fillMix`, the same two calls `ceiling.populateCell`
  makes — identical rock, one table.

SDF: two exact squared-Euclidean distance transforms (row pass + column pass),
one for the solid mask and one for its complement, over a chunk padded by the
band on both sides so the transform sees the neighbour's rock. Distances are
between cell centres, so half a cell is taken off: the zero level then lies on
the cell boundary a body collides with, instead of half a cell inside the rock.
`sdf = ±max(0, d·cell − cell/2)`, clamped to `±bandMeters`, negative inside.
Normals are the normalised central-difference gradient of `sdf`, computed on
demand (no per-normal storage).

Edits: `weaken` and `dig` set cells open, set `dug`, and refresh the SDF in the
`bandCells`-wide rectangle around the change by ring search — the same values the
chunk-wide transform produces, without rebuilding the chunk.

Streaming: a map of live chunks plus an `edited` set. A chunk outside the camera
window is dropped unless it was edited; an edited chunk is kept in a small store
and restored on return. Generation is capped at `maxChunksPerFrame` (1) after the
initial window, so a fast camera opens rock a frame ahead of the platform instead
of stalling.

Queries (no allocation, caller-owned scratch):

```
solidAt(x, y)               -> boolean
sdfAt(x, y)                 -> metres, bilinear over cell centres
normalAt(x, y, out)         -> unit outward normal
traceCircle(x, y, vx, vy, t, r, g, out) -> earliest contact {time,x,y,nx,ny}
floorBelow(x, y)            -> world y of the first solid top surface below
roofAbove(x, y)             -> world y of the first solid bottom surface above
spanAt(x, y)                -> void width in metres through that point
weaken(x, y, E, gain, radius) -> removed strength, cells pushed to `removed`
```

## 4. Game rules

Platform (P1): the existing machine, driven by its existing controller and
autopilot. Differences: piston range `±90°` instead of `±10°`, no side walls and
no chamber roof and no charge pad — the deck's own induction is the only recharge
the ball gets, the platform recharges at the cart dock (D10). Ground comes from
`floorBelow` at each wheel; a platform needs a `≥ 3.6 m` span, so it can never
rest on a knife edge (it slides off sloped ground by the existing pose solve).

Ball (P2): `{ kind: 'ball', id: 'ball', x, y, vx, vy, omega, battery, radius }`.
Commands: `left` / `right` motor (torque from the battery, reaction on the rock
via friction), `jump` (impulse along the contact normal, no energy created).
In the air the ball is a projectile; `omega` is carried, not driven.

Damage: every contact goes through `cave.weaken` from D3, with the chamber's
`gain`, `damageFalloffPower` and `hitLooseCap` so the numbers stay comparable.
A cell whose strength reaches zero is removed only if at least one of its four
cardinal neighbours is open (a free face on floor, wall or ceiling); strictly
interior rock and the `row = 0` / `row = rows - 1` section borders hold at
`heldCellShare` and await collapse or direct exposure.

Debris: one fragment per removed cell, the chamber's `spawnFragment` rules, but
resting on `sdfAt` instead of `floorAt`.

## 6. Invariants to test

1. Generation determinism: same seed, same chunk, byte-identical; a column on a
   chunk seam equals the neighbour's copy of it.
2. `sdf` sign and magnitude: solid `≤ 0`, void `≥ 0`, distance within `1e-6` of a
   brute-force reference inside the band; patch refresh equals a full rebuild.
3. `traceCircle` never returns a contact inside rock, and a dropped ball lands at
   `surface + radius`.
4. `weaken` never removes strictly interior rock or section border rows, and
   never removes more strength than the energy brings.
5. Energy: ball kinetic + rotational + potential + battery + heat + fracture is
   constant across a bouncy shot (the chamber's `totalEnergy` pattern).
6. Streaming: an edited chunk evicted and re-entered holds exactly its edits.
7. Debris rest: a fragment lands on the SDF surface at any orientation within one
   step, and the ledger balances.
