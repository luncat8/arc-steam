# 0.3.6 Solubility & Hydro-Erosion Model

A unified water–mineral chemistry and sediment-transport model for the 3-element `(R Metal, G Crystal, B Gas)` geology system (`js/minerals.js`, `js/geology.js`, `js/rock-materials.js`), paired with the Hybrid 2D fluid architecture (`0.3.6-Water-LLM-draft.md`) and validated interactively in `prototypes/solubility-lab.html`.

Deferred evaporation/crust precipitation and extended lab diagnostics are tracked separately in `solubility.2-plan.md`.

---

## 1. Design Principles & Role of the Water Gun

The water gun is **not** a second sandblaster or brute-force breaker. It occupies a distinct physical and economic niche:

1. **Very low mechanical fracture force:** Shooting intact insoluble rock (Stone, Iron, dry Chalcopyrite, Quartz, Corundum, Silver) barely chips it (`~0.05 J/droplet` vs. `3.0–7.8 J/cell`). Heavy breakers (ball, rockets, sandblaster) stay mandatory for hard veins and gas pockets.
2. **Fast, selective chemical dissolution with brine saturation:** Soluble salts and hydrates dissolve rapidly on exposed rock surfaces when bathed in unsaturated water. Water carries a finite dissolved capacity (`satCap`, default `60 g / water unit`), so fresh jet flow cuts cleanly while stagnant brine stalls until pumped away. Once leaching weakens a surface cell below structural collapse (`debrisLimit = 0.8 J`), probabilistic full-cell completion releases it cleanly:
	- **Soluble phase** enters the water as **per-mineral dissolved brine** (permanently suspended until recovered by the under-platform suction pump and credited to cargo at live market quotes).
	- **Volatile gas phase** erupts upward as rising bubbles and is **permanently lost** — expensive gas (`150` market cap) cannot be harvested wet.
	- **Insoluble solid phase** detaches and falls straight down as **solid debris** (collectible on the platform or eroded across the floor).
3. **Submerged soluble debris dissolves faster (`3×` surface area):** Crushed loose grains of soluble minerals sitting in unsaturated water dissolve chemically at `3×` the wall rate, rewarding combined dry-breaking + wet-leaching workflows while leaving insoluble grains untouched.
4. **Visible solution chemistry:** Dissolved minerals tint water continuously by their `dye` color so the player reads pool contents by sight before driving over to pump them:
	- **Halite / salt brine (`+G`)** tints water **emerald / jade green**.
	- **Blue Vitriol (`CuSO₄·5H₂O`, `+GB`)** tints water **cobalt / azure blue**.
	- Mixed brines blend smoothly into deep turquoise-teal.
5. **Separate mechanical hydro-erosion (bedload saltation):** Flowing water never dissolves insoluble debris. Instead, currents above a density-dependent pickup speed briefly lift and hop light grains downstream (`0.14–0.50 s` saltation hops) and drop them when flow slows — naturally washing light Quartz gangue away from heavy Gold nuggets.
6. **Unlimited v1 tank & under-platform recovery:** The water gun floods freely on power; the platform pumps water up from beneath its hull, booking dissolved solutes directly into cargo storage.
7. **Dual chemistry setting:** Both **Mineral Table (13)** (default) and **Composition Law `f(R, G, B)`** are supported as configurable settings. They share one saturation law, one gas-loss / destroyed-value ledger, one residue-quote rule (gas rock re-materialises at the stone quote), one dynamic debris-collapse gate, and one hydro-erosion solver. Mode B classifies high `B` into hydrate-bound (blue brine), crystal-bound (`55%` green brine + `45%` unleachable residue) and free (`85%` vented gas + `15%` stone residue); the split is exclusive, so Fluorite never vents gas in either setting.

## 2. Water's Place in the `(R, G, B)` System

**Decision: Water is an external fluid outside the dry mineral ledger, chemically acting as a polar `G+B` solvent.**

Keeping water outside the dry rock table avoids cargo-capacity conflicts with an unlimited v1 water tank and keeps the rock-mass conservation ledger exact. Chemically, treating water as a polar non-metal/volatile (`G+B`) carrier explains every mineral bond family in the `(R, G, B)` cube without arbitrary special cases:

| Element / Bond Family | Physical Meaning | Water Interaction |
|---|---|---|
| **`R–R` (Metallic)** | Dense metallic bonds (`Fe, Cu, Ag, Au`) | High specific gravity (`ρ` rises strongly with `R`); pure metals and dry sulfides are insoluble and resist hydro-erosion. |
| **`G–G` (Covalent Framework)** | Rigid crystal frameworks (`SiO₂`, `Al₂O₃`) | Hardest rock in the table (`6.0–7.8 J`) and chemically inert; loose light grains (`Quartz ρ = 2.6`, `Sand ρ = 1.6`) wash easily in current. |
| **`R··G` (Ionic Salt Pairs)** | Ionic metal–crystal lattices (`Halite`) | Polar `G+B` water pulls ionic pairs apart rapidly into green `+G` brine. |
| **`R+B+G` (Hydrated Salts & Excess `B`)** | Hydrates (`Blue Vitriol`) & trapped volatiles (`Fizzy Halite`, `Gas rock`) | Moderate `B` hydrates metal salts into fully soluble blue `+GB` solution. Excess `B` (`> 0.55`) cannot stay bound in wet solution: either stays locked inside an insoluble crystal skeleton (`Fluorite` leach floor) or **escapes upward as lost gas bubbles** (`Fizzy Halite`, `Gas rock`). |

## 3. Mapping Solubility onto the Real `(R, G, B)` Cube

Scanning the baseline 10-mineral table (`js/minerals.js`) across the 8 octants of `[0, 1]³` (`seed: 1..8`, `192 × 120` cells via `js/geology.js`) shows **four octants are largely empty pure `stone`**:

| Octant `(R, G, B)` | Baseline Dominant Minerals | Baseline Pure Stone | Chemical Meaning in the Solubility Model |
|---|---|---|---|
| `LLL` (low R, low G, low B) | Quartz, Iron, Sand | 7.0% | Common silicates, iron oxides, loose sand |
| `LLH` (low R, low G, high B) | Fluorite, Gas | 6.1% | Volatile gas pockets (`B` escapes wet) & leachable Fluorite-salt |
| `LHL` (low R, high G, low B) | Corundum, Quartz | 2.9% | Ultra-hard covalent crystals (immune to water) |
| `HLL` (high R, low G, low B) | Iron, Copper, Silver | 0.0% | Dense insoluble metals & dry Chalcopyrite sulfide |
| **`HLH` (high R, low G, high B)** | Edges of Copper & Gold only | **46.5%** | **Hydrated metal salt (`Blue Vitriol`, split from high-`B` Copper)** |
| **`HHL` (high R, high G, low B)** | Empty host stone | **69.8%** | **Ionic rock salt (`Halite`, `R·G` → `+G` green brine)** |
| **`LHH` (low R, high G, high B)** | Empty host stone | **62.1%** | Transition zone between Fluorite-salt, Quartz, and Fizzy salt |

Placing the new soluble phases in these naturally empty octants preserves every existing rare mineral's abundance (`corundum`, `gold`, `gas`, `sand`, `fluorite`) within `±0.02%` of baseline.

## 5. Dual Chemistry Settings: Mineral Table vs. Composition Law `f(R, G, B)`

Both models are exposed as a live game/lab setting (`solubilityMode: 'table' | 'formula'`) sharing the same fluid solver, saturation cap, structural collapse rule, submerged-grain chemistry, and hydro-erosion physics:

### Mode A: Mixture-Weighted Mineral Table (`'table'`, Default)
Each rock cell carries its normalized fractional mixture `mix[0..M-1]` from `js/geology.js`. Per-mineral `sol`, `ventF`, `leachFloor`, `residueF`, and `dye` weigh into the cell the same way `strengthInto()` weighs `strength`:
- Pure mineral seams execute their exact codex reactions cleanly.
- Mixed transition cells automatically dissolve only their soluble binder (e.g., `65% Halite + 35% Chalcopyrite`), dropping intact insoluble ore grains once the remaining skeleton drops below `debrisLimit`.
- Market pricing, cargo accounting, and rare minerals (`Gold`, `Corundum`, `Gas`) stay sharp and legible.

### Mode B: Continuous Composition Law (`'formula'`)
Computes reaction channels directly from raw cell `(R, G, B) ∈ [0, 1]³`. High `B` is split into **bound** and **free** fractions before any channel is assigned; the split is exclusive, so crystal-bound `B` can never vent:

```text
ionic        = smoothstep(0.22, 0.56, R * G)
hydrate      = smoothstep(0.15, 0.42, R * B * (1 - 0.65 * G))
degasRaw     = smoothstep(0.56, 0.86, B) * (0.35 + 0.65 * (1 - R * (1 - G)))
degas        = clamp(degasRaw * (0.42 + 0.48 * (1 - R)), 0, 1)
crystalB     = smoothstep(0.10, 0.30, G) * (1 - smoothstep(0.35, 0.50, R)) * smoothstep(0.55, 0.70, B)
hydrateB     = smoothstep(0.45, 0.62, R) * (1 - smoothstep(0.35, 0.55, G)) * smoothstep(0.50, 0.65, B)
boundB       = min(1, crystalB + hydrateB)
freeB        = max(0, 1 - boundB)
gasRock      = smoothstep(0.20, 0.40, B) * (1 - smoothstep(0.15, 0.30, G)) * (1 - smoothstep(0.25, 0.40, R)) * freeB
```

Raw channels:

```text
haliteBrine  = ionic * (1 - 0.35 * degasRaw)
blueBrine    = hydrate * (1 - 0.85 * ionic) + degas * hydrateB
freeGas      = degas * freeB
crystalBrine = 0.55 * crystalB
```

Apply crystal-bound `B` with `keepC = 1 - crystalB`: `haliteBrine = haliteBrine * keepC + crystalBrine`, `blueBrine *= keepC`, `freeGas *= keepC`. Apply free gas rock with `keepG = 1 - gasRock`: `haliteBrine *= keepG`, `blueBrine *= keepG`, `crystalBrine *= keepG`, `freeGas = freeGas * keepG + 0.85 * gasRock`. Mode B routes `crystalBrine` into the Fluorite brine channel and the remaining `haliteBrine` into Halite, so the `55/45` split is visible per mineral. If the reaction total exceeds `1`, normalise all four channels by the same factor. Then `solidResidue = max(0, 1 - reactionTotal)`, `solubility = reactionTotal`, `speed = (0.45 + 1.25 * reactionTotal) * smoothstep(0.02, 0.22, reactionTotal)` and `density(R, G, B) = clamp(2.0 + 7.5 * R * R - 0.8 * B, 1.2, 11.5)`. The smooth low-reactivity gate replaces a hard `0.22` cutoff.

At the calibrated centers: Blue Vitriol is `~0.91` total (`~0.90` blue brine, `<0.01` gas), Halite `1.00` ionic brine, Fluorite `0.55` green brine + `0.45` residue + `0` gas, Gas rock `0.85` gas + `0.15` stone residue, Fizzy `R₂G₂B₂` `~0.34` gas / `~0.66` brine, and dry Copper `0.00`. Tightening `hydrate` from `0.18–0.48` to `0.15–0.42` moves Vitriol from `0.676` toward `1.00` (`0.914` in the scalar plan formula, `0.907` in the channel law); it does **not** reach `1.00`. The earlier `~0.61` Gas / `~0.52` Fluorite totals describe only the scalar solubility formula, not the bound-B channel law.

## 6. Surface Dissolution, Saturation, Structural Collapse & Conservation

### 6.1 Saturation-Limited Dissolution Rate
Only exposed boundary cells (solid cells adjacent to fluid or struck by ballistic jet droplets) experience dissolution:

```text
sat(parcel)  = max(0, 1 - totalLoad(parcel) / max(eps, waterVol(parcel) * satCap))
chemRate     = baseDissolveRate * cellSolRate * sat(parcel) * (0.35 + 0.65 * min(1, contactFlow / 2.5))
```

- **Saturation cap (`satCap`):** Default **`60 g` dissolved solute per water unit** (slider-tunable in lab, tuned via yield probe for live game). Fresh jet water cuts at full speed (`sat = 1.0`); stagnant puddles saturate toward `sat = 0` and stall until the platform pumps the brine away; downstream salt seams survive a saturated runoff stream.
- **Mechanical fracture force:** Very weak (`~0.05 J/droplet` vs. `3.0–7.8 J` rock strength), chipping intact insoluble rock negligibly while still loosening soft `Sand` overburden (`0.5 J`).

### 6.2 Structural Skeleton Collapse via `strengthInto(mix)` + `debrisLimit`
Instead of an arbitrary solubility cutoff, cell detachment integrates directly with `js/rock-materials.js`:

1. For each wet surface cell, compute the residue skeleton strength:
	```text
	remStrength = sum over m of (insolubleFraction(m) * mix[m] * strength[m])
	```
	(For `fluorite`, the `45%` unleachable concentrate floor remains part of `insolubleFraction`.) A coherent residue of the same mineral keeps this static strength. An **inclusion seam** — soluble cement around different insoluble clasts — is undermined; its effective strength falls as the binder leaches and jet damage accumulates:
	```text
	effStrength = undermined ? max(0, remStrength * (1 - dissolveProg) - (mechMax - mechHp)) : remStrength
	```
2. **Collapse condition:**
	- If `effStrength < debrisLimit` (`0.8 J`), leaching undermines the cell's structural skeleton. As cumulative `rockDissolveProg[idx]` advances past `0.55`, each step rolls `rand() < (rockDissolveProg - 0.50) * 6 * dt` (guaranteed at `1.0`) for **probabilistic whole-cell detachment** — producing organic karst fronts while releasing all three phases cleanly:
		- **Brine share** enters the contacting water cell's per-mineral load vector `loads[m]`.
		- **Gas share (`ventF`)** spawns rising bubbles and books immediately to `lostGasMass` and `lostGasValue` (shown explicitly in the HUD/ledger; destroyed residue value is booked here too when gas rock re-materialises at the stone quote).
		- **Solid residue share** spawns falling solid debris grains carrying the exact remaining insoluble mass, mineral identity, density `ρ`, and quote. The detached grain values are scaled to the cell's remaining `rockValue` so cumulative slice rounding cannot break the exact ledger.
	- If `effStrength >= debrisLimit` (e.g., `15%` trace Halite inside `85%` solid Quartz, or pure `Fluorite` at `45% × 3.0 = 1.35 J`), only the accessible soluble binder can leach out without collapsing the intact insoluble framework (it stays standing until chipped mechanically or further undermined).
	- A `75%` Halite / `25%` Chalcopyrite seam has **static** residue strength `1.20 J`, above `debrisLimit`; it is not a static-collapse example. It collapses as an inclusion seam when binder loss and accumulated jet damage reduce `effStrength` below `0.8 J`, dropping Copper clasts.

### 6.3 Submerged Soluble-Grain Dissolution (`3×` Surface-Area Rate)
Loose solid debris grains of soluble minerals (`Halite`, `Blue Vitriol`, `Fizzy Halite`, `Gas rock`, and `Fluorite-Salt` down to its `45%` concentrate floor) submerged in water (`depth > 0.05`) continue to dissolve chemically at **`rate × 3.0 × sat(parcel)`** (reflecting higher surface area per unit mass):
- Follows the exact same stoichiometric splits (`brine`, `lostGas`, `leachFloor`, `stone` residue) and respects the water parcel's saturation cap.
- Once a fully soluble grain shrinks below threshold (`< 5 g`), the tiny remainder dissolves cleanly into the parcel with zero lost mass.
- Insoluble grains (`Stone`, `Iron`, `Chalcopyrite`, `Quartz`, `Corundum`, `Silver`, `Gold-quartz`, `Pure Gold`, `Sand`) never dissolve.

### 6.4 Per-Mineral Brine Bookkeeping & Under-Platform Suction
- Every fluid cell (`96 × 60`) and cascade droplet tracks dissolved load **per soluble mineral (`loads[m]`)** in `Float64Array` buffers (zero per-frame allocation, `< 1e-12` drift).
- Water visual color blends smoothly from fresh aqua `(44, 162, 214)` toward the load-weighted average of each dissolved mineral's `dye` color (`Halite` emerald `+G`, `Blue Vitriol` cobalt `+GB`, `Fizzy`/`Fluorite` mint-green `+G`, mixed teal).
- When the platform's under-hull suction draws a fraction `f = pulledWater / cellWater` from a water cell:
	- Exact shares `f * loads[m]` transfer into the platform hopper (`hopperLoads[m]`) and credit live market value `f * loads[m] * quote[m]`.
	- Solid debris is **never** drawn by the wet solute pump, preserving dry collection and placer panning as distinct mechanics.
- **Exact Conservation Invariant (checked to double-precision epsilon):**


## 7. Separate Process: Hydro-Erosion (Bedload Saltation) & Gold Panning

Hydro-erosion acts **only** on loose solid debris particles (never dissolving insoluble grains into brine):

### 7.1 Density-Sorted Pickup & Short-Hop Saltation
Each loose debris particle stores `(x, y, vx, vy, mineral, mass, value, density, state, hopTimer, panProg)` where `state ∈ {FALLING, RESTING, ENTRAINED}`:

1. **Critical pickup speed** scales linearly with submerged excess specific gravity `(ρ - 1.0)`:
	```text
	v_crit(ρ) = (0.78 / erosionPower) * max(0.35, ρ - 1.0)
	```
	At default `erosionPower = 1.0`:
	- `Sand (ρ = 1.6)` → `0.47 m/s` (washes easily in gentle trickle).
	- `Quartz (ρ = 2.6)` → `1.25 m/s` (hops briskly in moderate sluice stream).
	- `Stone (ρ = 2.8)` → `1.40 m/s` (short hops in brisk current).
	- `Chalcopyrite / Iron (ρ = 4.8–5.2)` → `2.96–3.28 m/s` (barely budges except in strong surges).
	- `Gold-bearing quartz (ρ = 5.6)` → `3.59 m/s` (stays mostly anchored on riffles while current washes off its light Quartz gangue).
	- `Silver (ρ = 10.5)` → `7.41 m/s` (stays where it drops).
	- `Pure Gold (ρ = 19.3)` → `14.27 m/s` (rock-solid anchor — concentrates cleanly in upstream riffles).
2. **Transient entrainment (NOT permanent dissolution):**
	- A `RESTING` grain submerged in flowing water (`depth > 0.05` and `|u| > v_crit(ρ)`) rolls per-second pickup chance `p_pick = min(0.95, (2.8 + 4.5 * (|u| / v_crit - 1)) * dt)`.
	- Once picked up, the grain becomes `ENTRAINED` for a short hop (`hopTimer ∈ [0.14, 0.50] s`, shorter for denser minerals), receives a small upward lift impulse, and tracks water velocity under submerged gravity `g_eff = g * (1 - 1 / ρ)`.
	- When `hopTimer <= 0` or flow drops below `0.65 * v_crit`, entrainment ends and the grain settles back to `FALLING → RESTING`.

### 7.2 Market-Consistent Placer Separation (`Gold-Bearing Quartz → Pure Gold + Plain Quartz`)
When a loose composite `gold` (`Au in SiO₂`, quote `147.35`) debris grain rests in active flowing water (`|u| > 0.55 m/s`) or direct jet wash:
- Agitation accumulates liberation progress (`panProg += |u| * erosionPower * 0.42 * dt`).
- Upon liberation (`panProg >= 1.0`), the grain splits with **exact mass and live-market value conservation**:
	- **Pure Gold nugget (`pure_gold`, `ρ = 19.3`)**: `25%` of lump mass, carrying **`97.35%`** of lump value (`~573.80 / cell` equivalent, `3.89×` gold-quartz per gram) — stays anchored right on the upstream riffles.
	- **Plain Quartz gangue (`quartz`, `ρ = 2.6`)**: `75%` of lump mass, priced directly at the live `quartz` quote (`5.21`, **`2.65%`** of lump value) — immediately entrained and washed downstream into the tailings basin.

This keeps live market accounting 100% exact without introducing a separate "gold-gangue quartz" commodity.
